On a site or library, select a file and choose Copilot to summarize, ask questions, or create content from approved documents.

GPrompting with GCSE▾

Every demo's first prompt is labeled with the four GCSE parts. After that, follow-ups are natural conversation in the same thread.

G

Goal

What outcome do you need?

C

Context

Who are you, what is at stake, and why is it hard?

S

Source

Which content should Copilot use, and which one is authoritative?

E

Expectation

What format, length, and handling of uncertainty do you want?

Progressive prompting in one example

VersionPromptWhy it is better
1. Vague"Summarize the supplier thread."Copilot has to guess the purpose, the source, and the format.
2. Better"Summarize the Project Luminous Teams thread for Thursday's meeting."Adds a purpose and a source.
3. GCSEGoal + Context + Source + Expectation (see Demo 01)Names the authority, the format, and asks Copilot to flag what it cannot verify.
4. Follow-up"Now challenge the summary..."Plain prose in the same thread; no need to repeat the labels.

01Find the signal in Teams▾

Move from conversation overload to a clean decision and action view.

📍 Copilot Chat (Work) · Teams content⏱ 5 min📈 Beginner

What it does. Teams is where current work unfolds: meetings, channel posts, questions, and informal decisions. Copilot extracts the operational signal while keeping uncertainty visible instead of turning every comment into a fact.

Who it is for. Supplier strategy leads, category managers, and program owners returning to a fast-moving issue after several days away.

Step 1 · Paste this first (or attach the file)

📋 Fictional Teams content
FICTIONAL TEAMS CONTENT
Workspace: Project Luminous - GSM Launch Readiness
Today: Monday 26 October 2026
Decision meeting: Thursday 29 October 2026, 2:00 PM Eastern

MEETING TRANSCRIPT EXTRACT - FRIDAY 23 OCTOBER

Maya Chen, Global Supplier Strategy Manager:
"We need one recommendation for Thursday: keep Aurelia as the primary decoration supplier, move the full volume to the alternate, or use a controlled dual-source plan. I do not want the group debating three different versions of the facts."

Elena Ruiz, Packaging Engineering:
"Aurelia's latest line trial passed visual inspection. The unresolved item is the 48-hour adhesion result. The lab expects that result Tuesday by noon."

Marcus Lee, Supply Planning:
"The launch requires 1.2 million decorated units. Aurelia has confirmed 850,000 units by the required ship window. Northstar Components can cover 350,000 units, but only if we reserve capacity by Wednesday at 3:00 PM."

Priya Nair, Responsible Sourcing:
"Aurelia's corrective action plan is still open. Two documentation items remain: subcontractor training evidence and the updated grievance log. Aurelia committed to send both by Tuesday at 5:00 PM."

Jon Bell, Finance:
"The dual-source model adds an estimated $0.08 per unit compared with the current plan. That estimate excludes expedited freight."

Maya:
"Marcus, reserve the Northstar capacity provisionally. Elena, bring the adhesion result. Priya, confirm whether the two documents close the corrective action or only advance it. I will prepare the decision brief."

CHANNEL POSTS - MONDAY 26 OCTOBER

08:12 - Marcus:
"Northstar confirmed the 350,000-unit hold through Thursday 29 October at 3:00 PM Eastern. No purchase commitment has been issued."

09:05 - Elena:

09:44 - Priya:

Step 2 · Starting prompt (GCSE)

✍ Starting prompt
Goal: Prepare me for the Thursday supplier decision meeting by summarizing what is decided, what is still open, and what must happen before the meeting.
Context: I am Maya Chen, the Global Supplier Strategy Manager for Project Luminous, and I need one shared view of a fast-moving supplier issue.
Source: Use only the fictional Teams meeting transcript and channel posts pasted above. Treat later channel posts as updates to earlier statements.
Expectation: Return a table with confirmed facts, open questions, action owner, deadline, and evidence from the source. Flag anything you cannot verify from the source.
What it does: Separates confirmed facts from open items and builds a meeting-readiness table. Why: Naming the source and telling Copilot that later posts update earlier statements prevents stale facts from winning.
💡 Did you know? In your real tenant, Copilot in Teams can recap a meeting only when transcription or recording was on. In Copilot Chat you can type / to reference a specific meeting, chat, or file you can already access.

Step 3 · Follow-ups in the same thread

↪ Follow-up 1
Now challenge the summary. What are the three statements someone might incorrectly repeat as settled facts, and what does the Teams record actually say?
What it does and why: Red-teams the recap so unresolved risks do not disappear inside a polished summary.
💡 Did you know? Asking Copilot to argue against its own answer is one of the fastest ways to surface weak assumptions. It costs one prompt and often saves a correction email.
↪ Follow-up 2
Draft a six-line Teams channel update in my voice. Lead with the Thursday decision, list the three time-critical dependencies, and do not imply that any open test or corrective action is complete.
What it does and why: Converts discussion into a concise update Maya can post back to the channel.
💡 Did you know? "In my voice" works better when Copilot knows your style. Custom instructions can store your preferred tone so you do not repeat it.
↪ Follow-up 3
What important decision inputs are not present in this Teams extract and therefore need another source before I make a recommendation?
What it does and why: Shows the value of asking Copilot to state the limits of its evidence.
💡 Did you know? Copilot answers only from content you can access. Asking what is missing is how you discover that the answer needs another source.
💡 Save as custom instruction: "Always separate confirmed facts from open questions, and flag anything you cannot verify from the source."
What you will see. Copilot identifies the Tuesday 4:00 PM adhesion result, Tuesday 5:00 PM document delivery, Wednesday review timing, and the Thursday 3:00 PM capacity hold. The payoff is correcting three tempting assumptions: the test passed, the corrective action closed, and the alternate capacity was purchased.

02Use SharePoint as the source of truth▾

Ground the supplier decision in approved documents, not just the latest conversation.

📍 Copilot Chat (Work) · SharePoint content⏱ 5 min📈 Beginner

What it does. SharePoint is the shared knowledge layer: controlled documents, approved standards, and team-owned records. Copilot uses authoritative material to test whether the Teams conversation is complete enough for a decision.

Who it is for. GSM strategy, responsible sourcing, procurement operations, and anyone who must distinguish approved guidance from discussion.

Step 1 · Paste this first (or attach the file)

Step 2 · Starting prompt (GCSE)

✍ Starting prompt
Goal: Evaluate the available supplier options against the approved Project Luminous decision criteria.
Context: I am Maya Chen preparing a supplier recommendation, and the SharePoint site is the shared source of truth for requirements, risk, and governance.
Source: Use the fictional SharePoint extract pasted above together with the Teams content already in this thread. Prefer the approved SharePoint documents for stable requirements and governance, and use Teams for later operational updates.
Expectation: Create a matrix for Aurelia primary, Northstar full transfer, and controlled dual source. For each option, show supporting evidence, unmet conditions, and what cannot yet be verified. Do not use the superseded draft.
What it does: Builds a decision-criteria matrix from the approved shared source and identifies evidence gaps. Why: Telling Copilot which source governs stable requirements and which provides recent updates mirrors how a careful analyst works.
💡 Did you know? In SharePoint and OneDrive you can select a file and ask Copilot to summarize it or answer questions without opening it. Copilot respects sensitivity labels and existing permissions.

Step 3 · Follow-ups in the same thread

↪ Follow-up 1
Explain why the superseded one-million-unit draft would produce a materially wrong answer. Keep the explanation practical for a GSM audience.
What it does and why: Makes Copilot explain why document status and location matter.
💡 Did you know? Superseded drafts that are still searchable are a common cause of wrong answers. Archiving or clearly labeling old versions improves Copilot results for everyone.
↪ Follow-up 2
Create a pre-read checklist from the governance standard. Phrase each item as a question the decision owner must be able to answer before the meeting.
What it does and why: Turns policy language into a reusable decision checklist.
💡 Did you know? Checklists phrased as questions are easy to reuse. Save this prompt so the team can run it before every governance meeting.
↪ Follow-up 3
Which facts came from Teams, which came from SharePoint, and which source should be updated after the decision so the final record is easy to find later?
What it does and why: Exposes the difference between a conversation update and an authoritative record.
💡 Did you know? Copilot responses grounded in your work content include citations. Select them to confirm exactly which file or message supported each claim.
💡 Save as custom instruction: "When sources conflict, prefer approved SharePoint documents for requirements and governance, and later Teams updates for operational timing."
What you will see. Copilot uses the approved 1.2-million-unit requirement, rejects the superseded draft, and treats the risk register and governance standard as the authority. Teams supplies freshness; SharePoint supplies durable control and shared meaning.

03Protect the OneDrive draft boundary▾

Use personal working content without promoting tentative ideas into team commitments.

📍 Copilot Chat (Work) · OneDrive content⏱ 5 min📈 Intermediate

What it does. OneDrive is where individual work begins: private notes, early drafts, and calculations. Copilot improves personal work while labeling unverified ideas and keeping the approved SharePoint record in control.

Who it is for. Anyone drafting a recommendation or using personal notes that are useful but not yet approved for team use.

Owner: Maya Chen
Folder: My Files / Project Luminous / Working

PERSONAL NOTES - 26 OCTOBER 2026

Step 2 · Starting prompt (GCSE)

✍ Starting prompt
Goal: Check my draft leadership note for unsupported, outdated, or misleading claims before I share it.

Step 3 · Follow-ups in the same thread

↪ Follow-up 1
In the rewrite, label the dual-source recommendation as my proposal and separate it from the conditions that still need verification.
What it does and why: Preserves useful personal thinking without confusing it with fact.
💡 Did you know? Labels such as 'proposal', 'assumption', and 'verified' are simple, but they make a draft much safer to forward.
↪ Follow-up 2
Show the cost calculation step by step. State which figure is supported, which figure is unsupported, and what cost elements remain unknown.
What it does and why: Mechanically checks the cost logic against the source numbers.
💡 Did you know? Asking for step-by-step math makes errors visible. For larger calculations, put the numbers in Excel and ask Copilot to build or explain the formula.
↪ Follow-up 3
Using the SharePoint information placement guide, tell me which parts of this OneDrive draft should remain personal working notes, which should be moved into the SharePoint decision record after approval, and which should be posted to Teams for immediate coordination.
What it does and why: Creates a clean handoff path from private work to shared team knowledge.
💡 Did you know? Where you save content changes who can find it. Copilot never widens access; publishing to SharePoint is how a draft becomes team knowledge.
💡 Save as custom instruction: "Treat my personal notes as proposals, not approved decisions, unless an approved source confirms them."
What you will see. Copilot catches the unsupported $96,000 figure, recalculates $28,000 (350,000 × $0.08), and corrects the false approval and capacity statements. OneDrive is a safe place to think, but not automatically the team's source of truth.

04Reconcile all three workspaces▾

Resolve conflicts by source role, status, and recency instead of blending everything together.

📍 Copilot Chat (Work)⏱ 5 min📈 Intermediate

What it does. Each location contributes a different kind of context. This demo teaches a repeatable hierarchy: approved SharePoint content for durable facts, later Teams updates for current operations, and OneDrive for your own analysis and draft language.

Who it is for. Cross-functional GSM teams preparing a recommendation from distributed conversations, documents, and personal work.

Step 1 · No new data

Continue in the same conversation. The content from earlier demos is the complete evidence set.

Step 2 · Starting prompt (GCSE)

✍ Starting prompt
Goal: Reconcile the Project Luminous evidence across Teams, SharePoint, and OneDrive into one decision-ready fact base.
Context: I am Maya Chen, and I need to prevent stale documents, informal discussion, and personal proposals from being blended into a false consensus.
Source: Use only the three fictional source blocks already pasted in this thread. Use approved SharePoint content for stable requirements and governance, later Teams posts for operational updates, and OneDrive only for my private analysis and draft.
Expectation: Return four sections: verified facts, current proposals, conflicts or corrections, and missing evidence. Cite each item by workspace and document or message name. Flag anything you cannot verify.
What it does: Creates one reconciled view while keeping provenance for every important statement. Why: Four labeled sections stop Copilot from blending facts, proposals, and gaps into a false consensus.
💡 Did you know? Staying in one conversation lets Copilot reuse everything already pasted. Starting a new chat would lose that context.

Step 3 · Follow-ups in the same thread

↪ Follow-up 1
For every conflict you found, explain which source wins and why. Use status, purpose, and recency rather than assuming the newest item is always authoritative.
What it does and why: Makes the source-resolution logic transparent enough for people to challenge it.
💡 Did you know? Newest is not always right. Status and purpose matter: an approved brief outranks a newer informal message about requirements.
↪ Follow-up 2
Create a traceability table for the launch volume, Aurelia capacity, Northstar capacity, incremental cost, adhesion status, responsible-sourcing status, and decision deadline.
What it does and why: Produces a compact traceability table for the decision record.
💡 Did you know? Copilot can turn any table into an Excel-ready format. Ask for CSV output when you want to paste it straight into a workbook.
↪ Follow-up 3
If a colleague could see the Teams channel but not the SharePoint risk register or my OneDrive notes, what parts of this analysis would be missing or less reliable? Do not guess about their permissions beyond this hypothetical.
What it does and why: Shows how access boundaries can change an answer without implying a system failure.
💡 Did you know? Two people can ask Copilot the same question and get different grounded answers because Copilot uses only what each person is permitted to see.
💡 Save as custom instruction: "Cite every important statement by workspace and document or message name."
What you will see. Copilot explains why the approved SharePoint brief controls the 1.2-million-unit requirement, why Monday Teams posts update timing, and why Maya's dual-source preference remains a proposal.

05Create the supplier decision brief▾

Turn grounded evidence into an executive recommendation without hiding conditions or uncertainty.

📍 Copilot Chat (Work), then Word⏱ 5 min📈 Advanced

What it does. Once the evidence is reconciled, Copilot shifts from retrieval to creation: a leadership-ready brief, meeting agenda, and spoken opening that keep facts, assumptions, and the recommendation clearly separated.

Who it is for. Supplier strategy leaders, meeting owners, and anyone who turns distributed work into a concise decision package.

Step 1 · No new data

Continue in the same conversation. The content from earlier demos is the complete evidence set.

Step 2 · Starting prompt (GCSE)

✍ Starting prompt
Goal: Draft a one-page decision brief for the Thursday Project Luminous supplier meeting.
Context: I am Maya Chen. My proposed direction is a controlled dual-source allocation, but the group must see the remaining technical, responsible-sourcing, capacity, and cost conditions before deciding.
Source: Use only the reconciled fictional evidence in this thread. Keep approved facts, current operational updates, and my proposal clearly separated.
Expectation: Write an executive brief with decision required, recommendation, rationale, option comparison, conditions and owners, financial impact, and explicit assumptions. Use concise business language and flag anything you cannot verify.
What it does: Produces a one-page decision brief with a recommendation that stays conditional on the evidence. Why: Listing the required brief sections gives Copilot a structure and makes missing information obvious.
💡 Did you know? With Office in the new Copilot app (October 2026), you can open the draft as a real Word document and keep editing alongside the conversation.

Step 3 · Follow-ups in the same thread

↪ Follow-up 1
Create a 20-minute meeting agenda with time boxes. Put unresolved evidence first, the option decision second, and ownership of post-decision updates last.
What it does and why: Converts the brief into a meeting structure focused on the actual decision.
💡 Did you know? Putting unresolved evidence first in an agenda prevents the meeting from rubber-stamping an incomplete decision.
↪ Follow-up 2
Write a 60-second opening statement in my voice. Make the decision, evidence boundary, and two most important conditions clear. Do not overstate supplier approval or capacity commitment.
What it does and why: Gives the presenter an opening that acknowledges uncertainty without sounding unprepared.
💡 Did you know? Sixty seconds is roughly 140 to 150 spoken words. Ask Copilot to hit a word count when you need a timed script.
↪ Follow-up 3
List the five hardest questions leadership may ask. For each, draft a grounded answer and state when the correct response is "we do not yet know."
What it does and why: Anticipates challenge questions and grounds the answers in available evidence.
💡 Did you know? "We do not yet know" is a credible executive answer when it comes with an owner and a date. Asking Copilot to use it reduces invented answers.
💡 Save as custom instruction: "In executive briefs, keep approved facts, current updates, and my proposals in separate sections, and state assumptions explicitly."
What you will see. Copilot creates a polished brief but keeps the Tuesday and Wednesday conditions, the provisional Northstar capacity, and the $28,000 supported estimate. The strongest moment is a credible "we do not yet know" answer.

✓Tips and Tricks▾

Start with GCSE

Goal, Context, Source, Expectation. The first prompt sets the frame; follow-ups can be plain conversation.

Stay in one thread

Copilot remembers everything in the current conversation. Start a new chat only when you change topics.

Name the source of truth

Tell Copilot which content governs (approved SharePoint documents) and which only updates it (later Teams posts).

Ask it to flag what it cannot verify

Add "Flag anything you cannot verify" to important prompts. It is the single best guard against confident guesses.

Use / to reference work content

In Copilot Chat, type / to pick a specific file, meeting, email, or person you can already access.

Attach or paste

Use + to upload a file, or paste text directly. Pasting is fastest for short excerpts and works well for practice.

Check the citations

Grounded answers include references. Select them to confirm the exact file or message behind a claim.

Challenge the answer

Ask: "What would someone wrongly repeat as settled?" or "What is the strongest counterargument?"

Ask for a format

Tables, checklists, six-line updates, CSV output, or a 60-second script. Format requests make answers usable immediately.

Use memory and custom instructions

In Copilot settings, Personalization lets you save preferences such as role, tone, and output format so you do not repeat them.

Save prompts you reuse

Save strong prompts and share them with your team so good practice spreads.

Summarize without opening

In OneDrive and SharePoint, select a file and ask Copilot for a summary or key points, or compare up to five files.

Meeting recaps need a transcript

Copilot in Teams meetings recaps best when transcription is on. Without it, Copilot can help only during the meeting.

Keep content tidy

Archive superseded drafts, keep owners current, and publish final records to SharePoint. Better content means better answers.

Dos and don'ts

✅ Do

  • Use only content you are authorized to access
  • Review and verify outputs before sharing
  • Name the authoritative source in your prompt
  • Label proposals and assumptions clearly
  • Keep sensitive data inside approved Microsoft 365 tools

⛔ Don't

  • Paste confidential data into unapproved consumer AI tools
  • Treat a draft or chat comment as an approved decision
  • Forward Copilot output without reading it
  • Assume Copilot can see content you cannot
  • Let Copilot make legal, sourcing, or financial decisions for you
💡 Starter custom instruction (paste into Copilot settings › Personalization)
I work in Global Supplier Management. When I ask for analysis, separate verified facts, proposals, and open questions. Prefer approved SharePoint documents for requirements and governance, and later Teams updates for timing. Use tables for comparisons, keep summaries concise, use American English, and flag anything you cannot verify.
