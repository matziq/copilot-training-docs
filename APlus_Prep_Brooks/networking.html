Networking

Networking is domain 2.0, 23% of Core 1, and the domain where a handful of memorized tables — ports, cable limits, and Wi-Fi standards — converts directly into points.

Ports and protocols

Memorize the port, transport, purpose, and failure symptom.

Port questions are free points if you drill them. The exam usually asks what service uses a port, which port must be opened, or which service is broken when traffic is blocked.

Port Protocol TCP/UDP What it does What breaks if it is blocked
20/21 FTP TCP Transfers files; 21 control, 20 data in active FTP. Legacy FTP uploads/downloads fail.
22 SSH TCP Secure remote shell. Secure command-line administration fails.
22 SFTP TCP Secure file transfer over SSH. Secure file transfers using SFTP fail.
23 Telnet TCP Unencrypted remote terminal. Legacy remote terminal access fails.
25 SMTP TCP Server-to-server mail transfer. Mail servers cannot relay outbound mail normally.
53 DNS UDP/TCP Resolves names to addresses; TCP also used for zone transfers and large replies. Names do not resolve, but direct IP access may still work.
67/68 DHCP UDP Assigns IP configuration; server 67, client 68. Clients fail to lease addresses and may get APIPA.
80 HTTP TCP Unencrypted web traffic. Plain web pages fail to load.
110 POP3 TCP Downloads email from a mailbox. POP mail clients cannot retrieve mail.
137–139 NetBIOS / NetBT TCP/UDP Legacy Windows name and session services. Legacy Windows browsing or name services fail.
143 IMAP TCP Synchronizes email folders without removing mail from server. IMAP clients cannot sync mail.
161/162 SNMP UDP Monitors and manages network devices; 162 receives traps. Monitoring systems stop collecting device status or alerts.
389 LDAP TCP/UDP Directory queries. Directory lookups and some authentication-related queries fail.
427 SLP TCP/UDP Service Location Protocol discovery. Automatic service discovery may fail.
443 HTTPS TCP Encrypted web traffic. Secure websites and many APIs fail.
445 SMB/CIFS TCP Windows file and printer sharing. Mapped drives, shared folders, and SMB printing fail.
548 AFP TCP Apple Filing Protocol. Legacy macOS file sharing fails.
587 SMTP submission TCP Authenticated email client submission. Email clients cannot send through the submission server.
993 IMAPS TCP IMAP over TLS. Secure IMAP mail sync fails.
995 POP3S TCP POP3 over TLS. Secure POP mail retrieval fails.
3389 RDP TCP/UDP Remote Desktop Protocol. Remote Desktop connections fail.
Most common ports

Memorize 22 SSH/SFTP, 25 SMTP, 53 DNS, 67/68 DHCP, 80 HTTP, 443 HTTPS, 445 SMB, and 3389 RDP first. Those appear constantly.

Exam trap

Telnet is port 23 and unencrypted. SSH is port 22 and encrypted. HTTP is port 80 and unencrypted. HTTPS is port 443 and encrypted.

IP addressing

Identify private ranges, APIPA, masks, gateways, and IPv6 basics.

IPv4 addresses are 32-bit addresses written as four decimal octets, such as 192.168.1.25. A host needs an IP address, subnet mask, default gateway, and DNS server to communicate normally on most networks.

Range Name What it means
10.0.0.0/8 Private IPv4 Used inside organizations; not routed on the public internet.
172.16.0.0/12 Private IPv4 Covers 172.16.0.0 through 172.31.255.255.
192.168.0.0/16 Private IPv4 Common home and SOHO range.
127.0.0.1 Loopback Tests the local TCP/IP stack on the same host.
169.254.0.0/16 APIPA Windows self-assigned address when DHCP fails.
What APIPA proves

An APIPA address proves the client did not receive a usable DHCP lease. It does not prove the NIC is bad. Check DHCP reachability, VLAN, cable, Wi-Fi association, switch port, and DHCP scope availability.

Subnet masks and CIDR

A subnet mask separates the network portion from the host portion. CIDR notation writes the number of network bits after a slash.

CIDR Mask Usable host cue Common use
/24 255.255.255.0 254 usable hosts Common small LAN.
/25 255.255.255.128 126 usable hosts Splitting a /24 in half.
/26 255.255.255.192 62 usable hosts Small department subnet.
/27 255.255.255.224 30 usable hosts Small office segment.
/28 255.255.255.240 14 usable hosts Very small subnet.
/30 255.255.255.252 2 usable hosts Point-to-point IPv4 link.

The default gateway is the router interface a host uses to reach other networks. Static addressing is manually assigned. DHCP automatically leases configuration from a scope. A DHCP reservation gives a device the same address from DHCP based on its MAC address.

IPv6 uses 128-bit addresses written in hexadecimal. Link-local addresses begin with fe80:: and are used on the local link. IPv6 has no broadcast; it uses multicast and neighbor discovery. SLAAC lets a host create its own IPv6 address using router advertisements.

Annotated ipconfig /all walkthrough
Ethernet adapter Ethernet:
   Description . . . . . . . . . . . : Intel Ethernet Adapter
   Physical Address. . . . . . . . . : 00-11-22-33-44-55
   DHCP Enabled. . . . . . . . . . . : Yes
   IPv4 Address. . . . . . . . . . . : 169.254.23.18
   Subnet Mask . . . . . . . . . . . : 255.255.0.0
   Default Gateway . . . . . . . . . :
   DNS Servers . . . . . . . . . . . : 192.168.1.1

Read it in order. DHCP is enabled, but the address is 169.254.x.x, so the client did not get a DHCP lease. The blank default gateway explains why it cannot reach other networks. DNS is listed, but name resolution may still fail because the host has no valid route.

If the address were 192.168.1.50 with mask 255.255.255.0 and gateway 192.168.1.1, then DHCP probably worked and you would move to DNS or upstream connectivity.

DNS and DHCP

Know name resolution, record types, DORA, and common failure patterns.

Name resolution commonly checks local cache first, then the hosts file, then the configured DNS server. If an IP address works but a name fails, suspect DNS before blaming the website.

Record Purpose Example use
A Maps a hostname to an IPv4 address. www to 203.0.113.10.
AAAA Maps a hostname to an IPv6 address. www to 2001:db8::10.
MX Identifies mail exchangers for a domain. Routes inbound email.
TXT Stores text values used for verification and mail security. SPF, DKIM, and DMARC records.
CNAME Creates an alias to another hostname. shop points to hosted provider name.
Symptom Likely cause What to check
Name does not resolve but IP works. DNS server, record, or client resolver issue. DNS server address, nslookup, cache, hosts file.
Some sites fail but others work. Bad cached record, filtering, upstream DNS issue, or domain-specific outage. Flush cache, try another resolver, check content filtering.
Wrong site loads. Wrong DNS record, poisoned cache, or hosts file override. Hosts file, DNS cache, authoritative record.

DHCP DORA

  1. Discover: The client broadcasts to find DHCP servers.
  2. Offer: A DHCP server offers an address and options.
  3. Request: The client requests the offered lease.
  4. Acknowledge: The server confirms the lease.

Scope exhaustion means the DHCP server has no available addresses to lease. A reservation is still managed by DHCP and is tied to a client identifier such as a MAC address. A static address is configured on the device itself. If the DHCP server is unreachable, Windows clients commonly self-assign APIPA addresses in 169.254.0.0/16.

Wireless

Memorize Wi-Fi generations, bands, channels, and interference.
Standard Band Max theoretical throughput Channel widths Typical range cue
802.11a 5 GHz 54 Mbps 20 MHz Shorter range, less legacy interference.
802.11b 2.4 GHz 11 Mbps 20 MHz Longer range, slow legacy standard.
802.11g 2.4 GHz 54 Mbps 20 MHz Longer range, more interference.
802.11n 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz 600 Mbps 20/40 MHz Better range with MIMO.
802.11ac 5 GHz 6.9 Gbps 20/40/80/160 MHz High throughput, shorter than 2.4 GHz.
802.11ax 2.4 GHz, 5 GHz, and 6 GHz for Wi-Fi 6E 9.6 Gbps 20/40/80/160 MHz Efficient in dense networks.

2.4 GHz travels farther and penetrates walls better, but it is crowded and has only three non-overlapping 20 MHz channels in the United States: 1, 6, and 11. 5 GHz is faster and cleaner but has shorter range. 6 GHz offers more channels and less congestion, but it has shorter range and requires Wi-Fi 6E or newer support.

MIMO uses multiple antennas to send or receive multiple streams. MU-MIMO lets an access point communicate with multiple clients more efficiently instead of serving one client at a time.

Interference sources include microwaves, cordless phones, Bluetooth devices, neighboring access points, thick walls, metal shelving, elevators, and poorly planned channel overlap. Place APs high, central, and away from major obstructions. Use channel planning so neighboring APs do not fight on the same channel.

Technology Range Use case Exam cue
Bluetooth Personal area, commonly about 10 m for many devices. Headsets, keyboards, mice, speakers. Short-range peripheral pairing.
NFC A few centimeters. Tap-to-pay, badge tap, quick pairing. Very close proximity.
RFID Varies from centimeters to many meters. Inventory tags, badges, asset tracking. Tag identification.
Long-range fixed wireless Miles with line of sight. Building-to-building or rural ISP links. Directional antennas and line of sight.

SOHO networks

Configure a small router securely and know the feature tradeoffs.

A SOHO router usually combines router, switch, access point, firewall, DHCP server, and NAT in one box. The setup goal is simple: secure the admin plane, secure Wi-Fi, segment guests, and expose only what is required.

  1. Change the default administrator username and password.
  2. Update firmware before placing the router into normal service.
  3. Set a clear SSID; disable SSID broadcast only when policy requires it, because it is not strong security by itself.
  4. Use WPA3-Personal when available, or WPA2-AES if WPA3 is unavailable.
  5. Do not use WEP or WPA/TKIP; both are obsolete and insecure.
  6. Create a guest network with client isolation so guests cannot reach internal systems.
  7. Size the DHCP scope for expected clients and set reservations for infrastructure devices.
  8. Use port forwarding only for required inbound services.
  9. Disable UPnP unless there is a documented need.
  10. Use content filtering and firewall rules to enforce the organization’s policy.
  11. Use a DMZ only when a host must be exposed; understand that it increases risk.
  12. Save and document the configuration after testing.
Feature What it does Exam trap
Port forwarding Always forwards a specific inbound port to an internal host. Creates a persistent exposure.
Port triggering Temporarily opens inbound traffic after an outbound trigger. Dynamic and usually client-initiated.
UPnP Lets devices request port mappings automatically. Convenient but risky if malware abuses it.
DMZ Places a host outside normal firewall protection or forwards most unsolicited inbound traffic to it. Not the same as a secure enterprise DMZ design.

Network hardware

Know what each device does and what failure looks like.

Router

Routes traffic between networks. If it fails, clients can reach local devices but not other networks or the internet.

Switch

Connects devices within a LAN using MAC addresses. If it fails, wired LAN clients lose local connectivity.

Managed switch

Supports VLANs, monitoring, trunking, and configuration. Misconfiguration can isolate ports or break VLAN access.

Unmanaged switch

Basic plug-and-play switching. If it fails, connected devices lose link or cannot pass traffic.

Access point

Provides Wi-Fi bridge access to the wired network. If it fails, wireless clients disconnect while wired clients may still work.

Firewall

Filters traffic by rules. If misconfigured, allowed services are blocked or unwanted traffic is exposed.

Patch panel

Terminates building cabling in a wiring closet. Bad punchdowns cause intermittent or dead wall jacks.

PoE switch/injector

Provides power over Ethernet to APs, phones, and cameras. If the power budget is exceeded, devices may not boot or may reboot under load.

Hub vs switch

A hub repeats traffic to all ports; a switch forwards intelligently. Hubs cause collisions and are legacy.

Cable modem

Connects a customer network to a cable ISP over coax. Failure causes loss of ISP link while local LAN may remain up.

DSL modem

Connects through telephone lines. Failure or missing filters can cause no sync or unstable service.

ONT

Optical network terminal converts fiber service to customer Ethernet or voice. Failure drops fiber internet service.

NIC

Network interface card provides wired or wireless network access. Failure causes no link, driver errors, or poor throughput.

SDN

Software-defined networking centrally controls network behavior. Controller issues can affect many devices at once.

Load balancer

Distributes client requests across servers. Failure causes application outages or uneven server load.

Proxy server

Intermediates client web or application traffic. Failure causes browsing or app access issues even when direct network access works.

IDS vs IPS

IDS detects and alerts; IPS can block. A bad IPS rule can stop legitimate traffic.

Spam gateway

Filters inbound and outbound email. Failure can allow spam through or block legitimate mail.

Network tools

Map the exact scenario to the exact tool.

This section trips people up because the answer is usually the tool, not the theory. Read the scenario and pick what a technician would physically use next.

Tool What it is for Exam scenario
Crimper Attaches RJ45 plugs to twisted-pair cable. You made a patch cable and need to terminate the plug.
Cable tester Checks continuity, shorts, opens, crossed pairs, and miswires. A new cable run does not work and you need to verify pinout.
Wire stripper Removes outer jacket without damaging conductors. You are preparing cable before termination.
Punchdown tool Seats wires into IDC terminals on patch panels and keystone jacks. You are terminating a wall jack or patch panel.
Toner probe Traces a cable through walls or bundles. You need to find which cable in the closet goes to a specific office.
Loopback plug Tests a network port’s transmit/receive function. You need to test whether a NIC or switch port can loop traffic.
Network tap Copies traffic for analysis without disrupting the link. You need to capture packets between two devices.
Wi-Fi analyzer Shows SSIDs, signal strength, channels, and interference. Wireless is slow and you suspect channel overlap or weak signal.
Multimeter Measures voltage, resistance, and continuity. You need to verify electrical power, PoE voltage, or a basic continuity condition.
Tool trap

Use a cable tester to verify a cable. Use a toner probe to find a cable. Use a punchdown tool for jacks and patch panels. Use a crimper for RJ45 plugs.
