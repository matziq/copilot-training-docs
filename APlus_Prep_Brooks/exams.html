Take these properly or do not take them at all

A practice exam taken with your phone next to you, in three sittings, with the clock paused and a browser tab open, tells you nothing except that you can look things up. Sit down, put the phone in another room, close every tab, start the clock, and do not stop. The score you get under real conditions is the only number worth having — and it is the number the whole plan is calibrated against.

Three is the right number

More would not help. Full-length exams are expensive in time and energy, and they measure far more than they teach.

What raises a score is the day after the exam, when you work out why each miss happened. Three exams give you three full repair cycles, and each cycle points the next two days of work at something specific rather than at “more studying.”

On the days in between, use the topic drills. Same benefit, ten minutes instead of an hour.

Exam-day habits to rehearse now

Pacing

  • One minute per question. On the real exam that is 90 questions in 90 minutes.
  • Be at question 30 by minute 30 and question 45 by minute 45. Check twice, not constantly.
  • Performance-based questions come first and should be done last. Flag them, clear the multiple choice, then return with whatever time is left.
  • If a question passes 90 seconds, answer it, flag it, and move. You can come back.
  • Leave five minutes at the end to sweep your flagged questions.

Reading

  • Read the last line first. BEST, FIRST, NEXT, and MOST LIKELY each change which answer is correct.
  • “What should the technician do NEXT” almost always means the next step of a documented process, not the cleverest fix.
  • Watch for “Select TWO” and “Select THREE.” Missing one costs the whole question.
  • Two choices that mean the same thing are usually both wrong.
  • Never leave anything blank. Four choices, no penalty, so a blind guess is worth 25%.

How the score is estimated

CompTIA scores the A+ from 100 to 900 and passes Core 1 at 675 and Core 2 at 700. It has never published the raw-to-scaled conversion, and the real exam weights questions by difficulty, so no practice test anywhere can produce your exact score.

What this site does is anchor a curve on the two facts that are public — the 100 to 900 range and the passing marks — so that 72% correct lands on 675 and 75% lands on 700. That makes the number honest as a readiness signal even though it is not an official score.

Read it this way, against your exam’s mark: 25+ above means you are ready, at or just above means you would probably pass but there is no margin, up to 60 below means two focused days would get you there, and further below means the domain breakdown under your score is where all your attention should go.
