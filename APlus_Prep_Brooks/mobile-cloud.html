Mobile Devices, Virtualization & Cloud

These are domains 1.0 (13%) and 4.0 (11%) of Core 1 — together about a quarter of the exam, and both are heavy on vocabulary rather than hands-on skill, which makes them cheap points if the terms are memorized.

Laptop hardware and replaceable parts

Know which part fixes which symptom before you open the case.

Laptop repair is about replaceable modules. CompTIA expects you to identify the most likely failed part, not to describe every screw. Start with model-specific documentation, remove power, disconnect the battery when possible, and protect small ribbon cables.

!

Battery

A swollen battery is a safety problem, not a calibration problem. Power down, stop using the device, do not puncture or compress it, and send it to approved recycling or vendor disposal.

R

RAM

Laptop memory uses SODIMM modules. Ultrabooks, tablets, and many thin business laptops may have soldered memory that cannot be upgraded separately.

S

Storage

Older laptops commonly use 2.5-inch SATA drives. Modern systems commonly use M.2 SSDs; the connector shape does not automatically tell you whether the drive is SATA or NVMe.

The wireless card is usually a small M.2 or mini PCIe module. Its antenna leads route through the display hinge and into the display bezel, so weak Wi-Fi after a screen repair often points to a loose antenna lead, not the access point.

Input and media parts are also modular: keyboard, touchpad, webcam, microphone, speakers, plastics, frames, and the system board. A digitizer senses touch; the display panel creates the image; an inverter powers an older CCFL backlight.

Repair habit Photograph screw locations and cable routing before removal. Laptop screws may be different lengths, and one long screw in the wrong hole can crack a palm rest or display back cover.
Symptom Part to suspect Why it fits
Battery bulges, case separates, trackpad rises Battery Swelling means gas buildup inside the cell pack; stop use and replace safely.
System shuts off when unplugged Battery or DC jack If it runs on AC but not battery, test battery health and charging circuitry.
Several adjacent keys fail Keyboard A matrix row or ribbon cable can fail while the rest of the laptop works.

Laptop displays and special features

Separate panel type, lighting, touch, and external expansion.

LCD panel terms describe the image technology. IPS usually gives better viewing angles and color. TN is inexpensive and fast but has weaker viewing angles. VA has strong contrast but can show slower pixel response. OLED lights each pixel individually and can deliver deep black levels, but burn-in risk is a classic tradeoff.

The backlight is the light source behind an LCD. Older CCFL panels use an inverter; modern LED-backlit LCD panels usually have LED driver circuitry instead. If a flashlight shows a faint desktop on a dark screen, the image path works and the lighting path is suspect.

D

Docking station

A docking station expands the laptop into a desktop setup with power, displays, network, audio, and USB devices. It may use USB-C, Thunderbolt, or a proprietary connector.

P

Port replicator

A port replicator mainly duplicates ports for convenience. On the exam, a docking station is the richer expansion answer; a port replicator is the simpler connection hub answer.

Wi-Fi antenna connectors are small snap-on leads near the wireless card. The leads are normally labeled main and auxiliary, then routed up through the hinge into the display bezel for better radio performance.

Camera, webcam, and microphone components are often grouped near the top display bezel. A privacy shutter, firmware setting, application permission, or driver issue can look like a hardware failure, so check the simple controls before replacing the module.

Touchscreen repairs require correct vocabulary. The digitizer detects touch input, the display panel presents the image, and the bezel or glass may be separate plastics. Replacing the wrong layer is an expensive exam trap.

Mobile connectivity

Memorize connector capabilities and Bluetooth order.
C

USB-C

USB-C is reversible and can carry power, USB data, audio, and video using alternate modes such as DisplayPort. Capability depends on the port, cable, and device.

L

Lightning

Lightning is Apple proprietary. It carries power and data and can support audio or video through adapters, but it is not a generic USB-C connector.

M

Micro-USB

Micro-USB is legacy, not reversible, and typically carries charging and USB 2.0 data. It usually needs adapters for video or special accessories.

Bluetooth pairing order

The exam likes this sequence because it is operational, not theoretical. Keep it exact.

  1. Enable Bluetooth on the mobile device.
  2. Enable pairing or discovery mode on the accessory.
  3. Find the device in the mobile Bluetooth list.
  4. Enter or confirm the PIN, passcode, or pairing prompt.
  5. Test connectivity by using the accessory.
Bluetooth trap Do not test first and do not enter a PIN before discovery. Pairing is radio on → accessory discoverable → select device → PIN → test.

NFC is short-range wireless. It is used for tap-to-pay, badge access, quick pairing, and small data exchanges. Its short range is a feature because it reduces accidental long-distance communication.

A hotspot turns the phone into a Wi-Fi access point for other devices. Tethering shares the phone connection with one device over USB, Bluetooth, or Wi-Fi. A hotspot is a common form of tethering, but tethering is the broader term.

Airplane mode disables radios for flight safety and troubleshooting. Users can often re-enable Wi-Fi or Bluetooth manually while airplane mode remains on, depending on the platform.

Mobile application and email configuration

Ports, profiles, and synchronization are recall points.

Personal email setup usually needs a username, password, mail server names, ports, encryption settings, and whether the account is POP3 or IMAP. Corporate email may add Exchange, ActiveSync, mobile device management, certificate trust, VPN, and security policy prompts.

Protocol Port What to remember
IMAP 143 Receives mail while leaving it synchronized on the server.
IMAP over SSL/TLS 993 Secure IMAP; this is the mobile-friendly receive option.
POP3 110 Downloads mail; less useful for multi-device synchronization.
POP3 over SSL/TLS 995 Secure POP3.
SMTP 25 Server-to-server mail transfer; often blocked for client sending.
SMTP submission 587 Authenticated client submission, commonly with STARTTLS.
SMTPS 465 SMTP over SSL/TLS; still appears in exam port questions.
Exchange ActiveSync 443 Corporate mail, calendar, contacts, policy, and remote management over HTTPS.
S/MIME Uses certificates Encrypts and digitally signs email; users need the right certificate installed.
2

Two-factor authentication

Mobile 2FA may use push approval, authenticator codes, SMS, phone calls, or hardware keys. App passwords may be required for legacy apps that cannot handle modern authentication.

M

MDM vs MAM

MDM manages the device: passcode, encryption, remote wipe, Wi-Fi, VPN, and certificates. MAM manages the application and its data, often without controlling the entire device.

Enrollment usually installs a profile. That profile can configure corporate email, Wi-Fi, VPN, certificates, restrictions, and approved applications. Corporate application repositories let employees install approved internal apps without using a public app store.

Synchronization may include mail, calendar, contacts, photos, files, and bookmarks. Sync to cloud keeps data in a provider account such as iCloud, Google, OneDrive, or Exchange. Sync to desktop copies data to a local computer through a cable or desktop app.

Account requirements matter. The user must sign in to the correct account, have permission to sync the data type, and have storage capacity available. Data caps also matter because photo and file synchronization can burn cellular data quickly.

Port memory Receive mail: IMAP 143 or 993, POP3 110 or 995. Send mail: SMTP 25, 587, or 465. Secure versions are the larger numbers in the IMAP and POP3 pairs.

Virtualization

Know the hypervisor layer, resource needs, and risks.
Type Where it runs Examples Where it belongs
Type 1, bare-metal Directly on hardware VMware ESXi, Microsoft Hyper-V, Xen, KVM-based platforms Servers, data centers, production virtualization
Type 2, hosted On top of a host operating system VirtualBox, VMware Workstation, VMware Fusion, Parallels Desktop Technician labs, desktops, test VMs, training
Most-missed distinction Type 1 does not mean cloud and Type 2 does not mean desktop hardware. The distinction is the layer: Type 1 runs on hardware; Type 2 runs inside a host OS.

Resource requirements start with a CPU that supports virtualization extensions such as Intel VT-x or AMD-V. Those extensions may need to be enabled in UEFI. Without them, a VM may fail to start or run only limited features.

RAM must be sized for the host plus every running guest. Storage must hold virtual disks and snapshots. Network settings decide whether the VM is isolated, bridged onto the LAN, or hidden behind NAT.

Security requirements include patching the host, patching the guest, protecting VM images, using least privilege, and isolating risky test machines. A VM escape is an attack where code breaks out of the guest and affects the host or other guests.

VM sprawl happens when teams create many VMs and forget ownership, patching, cost, or purpose. Sprawl wastes storage and creates unmanaged attack surface.

S

Sandboxing

Run suspicious software or risky configuration changes in an isolated VM instead of on a production workstation.

T

Test development

Build repeatable test systems, snapshots, and clean rollback points for developers and support teams.

L

Legacy support

Run an older OS or application when the current hardware or operating system no longer supports it directly.

Application virtualization separates an application from the local OS so it can be delivered, isolated, or streamed more easily. Cross-platform virtualization lets a user run software built for a different operating system, such as Windows software on a macOS host.

Cloud computing

Draw the service split and memorize the named characteristics.
Model Provider manages You manage Concrete example
IaaS Data center, physical servers, storage, networking, virtualization layer Operating system, patches, applications, data, identity decisions A rented cloud VM running Windows Server or Linux
PaaS Infrastructure, OS, runtime, scaling platform, managed database or app service Application code, data, configuration, access rules A managed web app platform or database service
SaaS Almost the whole application stack and service operation User accounts, data usage, settings, and access policy Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, Salesforce, Dropbox

Public cloud

Provider-owned services delivered over the internet to many customers.

Private cloud

Cloud-style resources dedicated to one organization, on-premises or hosted.

Hybrid cloud

Public and private resources connected so workloads or data can move between them.

Community cloud

Shared by organizations with a common mission, compliance need, or industry requirement.

Characteristic Plain meaning Exam phrase to connect
Shared resources Multiple customers or workloads use pooled compute, storage, and network resources with logical separation. Resource pooling and multi-tenancy.
Rapid elasticity Capacity can expand or shrink quickly as demand changes. Scale out during peak demand, scale in when demand drops.
Metered utilization Usage is measured for billing, reporting, chargeback, or capacity planning. Pay for what you use.
High availability Services are designed to keep running through component failures using redundancy. Uptime, failover, resilient design.
File synchronization Files update across devices through a cloud account. OneDrive, iCloud, Google Drive, Dropbox style behavior.

VDI on-premises runs virtual desktops from company-controlled servers. VDI in the cloud delivers desktops from a cloud provider, which can reduce local infrastructure but depends on internet connectivity, identity, licensing, and provider capacity.

Desktop virtualization is the broader idea of separating the desktop environment from the physical endpoint. VDI is one implementation. A local Type 2 VM running a second desktop OS is also desktop virtualization, but it is not cloud VDI.

Cloud trap SaaS is not just a website. SaaS means the provider operates the application for you. IaaS gives you the VM; PaaS gives you the runtime; SaaS gives you the finished app.
