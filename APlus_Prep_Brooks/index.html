Which one are you working on today?

The A+ is not one test. Core 1 and Core 2 are separate appointments, separate fees, and separate scores, and you are only certified after passing both. How the two exams fit together

The rules

  • Both exams must be passed to earn the certification. There is no partial credit and no single-exam version of the A+.
  • You can take them in either order, and on different days. Most people take Core 1 first because it is closer to the hardware knowledge people already have.
  • They are priced and booked separately, and each is 90 questions in 90 minutes.
  • Core 1 passes at 675. Core 2 passes at 700 — a genuinely higher bar, not a rounding difference.
  • Once you pass the second one, the certification is good for three years from that date.

They fail for different reasons

Core 1 is mostly recall with one big reasoning domain. People fail it by not memorizing the tables — ports, RAID minimums, cable limits, DDR pin counts.

Core 2 is more evenly spread, and people fail it by skipping Operational Procedures because it is “not technical.” That domain is 21% of the exam and is pure list-memorization with no hardware knowledge required. It is the cheapest 21% on the entire A+.

The other Core 2 trap is order-of-operations questions: the seven-step malware removal process and the change-management sequence get tested literally.

If you are deciding what to do first

Take a cold practice exam before you study anything

On whichever track you pick, day one is a full practice exam taken before you open a single guide. It will feel bad and the score does not matter. Its only job is to tell you which two domains are actually costing you points, because the rest of the plan is built around that answer.

Studying first and then testing tells you nothing. You will have revised the things you already knew and confirmed you know them.

Do not study for both at once

Pick the exam with the nearer date, work only that track until you sit it, then switch. The two exams share almost no content, so alternating between them costs you the momentum on both. The switcher in the header is there so the site follows that decision, not so you can bounce between them daily.
