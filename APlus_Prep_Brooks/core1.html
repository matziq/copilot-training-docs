Where the points are

Bars are drawn relative to the largest domain, not to 100% of the exam.

What that means for nine days

Troubleshooting and Hardware are 53% of the exam. If you knew nothing else and got those two perfect, you would already be close to the line.

They also fail in opposite ways. Hardware is pure recall — pin counts, RAID minimums, cable limits — so it responds instantly to flashcards. Troubleshooting is scenario reasoning, so it only responds to doing questions and reading why you were wrong.

Strategy in one line

Flashcards for Hardware and Networking. Practice questions for Troubleshooting. Do not mix the two methods up — reading a troubleshooting chapter feels productive and teaches almost nothing.

What 675 actually costs you in questions

CompTIA does not publish its raw-to-scaled conversion, but 675 of 900 lands close to 72% correct. On a 90-question exam that is about 65 right and 25 wrong.

The gap between failing and passing is smaller than it feels

Sixty-five right out of ninety. That is the whole job. It means you can miss an entire quarter of the exam and still walk out certified — so stop trying to learn everything and start closing the specific gaps the practice exams find. Six extra correct answers is the difference between 660 and 720.

How the next nine days work

The nine days ending the day before your exam.

1

Test first, study second

Day 1 is a full practice exam before you have studied anything. It will feel bad. It is also the only way to find out which of the five domains is actually costing you points, and the whole plan is built on that answer.

2

Three exams, three repairs

Practice exams on days 1, 4, and 7. Each one is followed by a repair day aimed squarely at what it exposed. Testing measures; reviewing improves.

3

Taper, do not cram

Nothing new after day 8. The last day is flashcards, the error log, and sleep. Cramming the night before reliably lowers scores.

Why the review matters more than the exam

Taking a practice exam tells you your score. It does not raise it. The gain comes from the hour afterward, when you work out why each miss happened — a fact you never learned, a fact you knew but read past, or a question you misread. Sort every miss into one of those three buckets and the fix becomes obvious. The error log exists for exactly this.

Exam day logistics

The night before

  • Confirm the appointment time and whether it is in-center or online proctored — they have completely different rules.
  • Two forms of ID, at least one government-issued with a photo and signature. Names must match the booking exactly.
  • If it is online proctored: run the system test, clear the desk down to bare wood, and check that nothing is on the walls.
  • Print or screenshot the confirmation email with the exam code 220-1101 on it.
  • Stop studying by 8:00 pm. Lights out early.

The morning of

  • Eat protein, not just sugar. Do not skip breakfast.
  • Arrive thirty minutes early. Late arrivals get turned away and lose the fee.
  • Warm up on ten flashcards in the car. Do not learn anything new.
  • Do the performance-based questions last. Flag them, clear the multiple choice, then come back with the time you have left.
  • If a question passes 90 seconds: answer it anyway, flag it, move on.
  • Read the last line first. BEST, FIRST, and MOST LIKELY change the answer.
  • Answer every single question. There is no penalty for being wrong.
