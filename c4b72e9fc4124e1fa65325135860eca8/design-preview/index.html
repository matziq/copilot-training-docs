What I would change.

These recommendations come from the current repository and local desktop/mobile rendering. They are not claims about analytics or measured conversion rates.

  1. FIRST PRIORITY

    Lead with the next race.

    The opening promotes 2025 results and a long record-breaking recap before the 2026 registration action. The page asks a new visitor to understand last year before learning this year's essentials.

    Evidence: index.html, Home section. At 390px wide, the first explicit race-date paragraph in the pricing content sits roughly 1,271px down the page.

    Change: Put “Thanksgiving, November 26, 2026,” “5K run/walk,” “Paul, Idaho,” and “9 AM” in the opening. Give registration one dominant treatment. Move the 2025 record into concise supporting proof.

  2. FIRST PRIORITY

    Turn the navigation into a short list of decisions.

    Twelve equal-weight navigation pills compete with each other. Travel and Visit overlap, while registration is visually similar to secondary destinations.

    Evidence: 12 links in the desktop navigation; local desktop capture shows the floating mascot competing with the right end of the header.

    Change: Use Race day, Community, FAQs, and Results, plus a distinct Register button. Group travel, parking, and the course under race planning. Keep volunteering, sponsors, photos, and contact discoverable below.

  3. FIRST PRIORITY

    Make mobile comfortable to read.

    The current mobile opening shrinks the logo, headline, recap, and actions. The recap becomes a dense block of small text rather than a quick invitation.

    Evidence: local 390 × 844 screenshot; the body copy and action labels are visibly small compared with the header and floating controls.

    Change: Use approximately 16–18px body copy, generous line spacing, fewer words, and touch-friendly controls. Keep a compact mobile registration bar, with enough bottom padding that it does not hide the final content.

  4. FIRST PRIORITY

    Let chat help only when asked.

    The local mobile preview exposes a large blank Chat panel over the page. On desktop, the mascot control competes with the navigation. Even useful support is a distraction if it arrives before the visitor understands the race.

    Evidence: local desktop and mobile captures. This is an observed layout/startup symptom, not a diagnosis of the remote chatbot service.

    Change: Start collapsed, show a small clearly labeled Help button, and open chat only on request. Constrain its size and stacking, provide a visible close control, and check loading/error states. Keep FAQs available without chat.

  5. DESIGN PRIORITY

    Use your people as the visual identity.

    The homepage's opening is text-heavy despite the repository containing authentic, expressive community photography. Repeated mascot images, gradients, shadows, and pills compete for attention.

    Evidence: Home section and existing media assets, including lucky-at-finish-line.jpg, mctt-starting-line.jpg, and mctt-2025.jpg.

    Change: Lead with one great race image or one deliberate mascot composition. Adopt a small palette, a clear heading/body type pairing, and fewer container styles. Use open space and hierarchy instead of boxing every block.

  6. CONTENT PRIORITY

    Shorten the homepage without losing useful information.

    Registration, travel, local attractions, volunteer instructions, results, testimonials, sponsors, FAQs, gallery, and contact all sit in one long document.

    Evidence: current page measures about 16,974px tall at 1440px wide and 23,668px at 390px wide in the local browser.

    Change: Keep the homepage to the invitation, essential race plan, community proof, compact pricing, common questions, and a final action. Move detailed planning and archives to focused destinations; retain links to everything.

  7. TECHNICAL PRIORITY

    Give event facts one source of truth.

    The active schedule and FAQ say 9 AM. The currently unreferenced assets/js/index.js countdown targets 8:30 AM. Pricing, dates, and long page content are also repeated across the static routes and React stack.

    The homepage comments out the countdown script, so this is a maintenance inconsistency, not a live countdown failure.

    Change: Centralize the event year, date, timezone, start, venue, fees, and registration URL. Generate the static route copies from shared source. Keep the deployed static stack authoritative until the Next.js migration is actually released.

  8. TECHNICAL PRIORITY

    Improve semantics and load only what is needed.

    The current homepage has no main landmark. MapLibre is loaded in the head, and the document contains 53 images and 12 script elements. Several anchor offsets are negative in the shared CSS.

    Evidence: current DOM; assets/css/styles.css anchor rules; index.html head and script includes. No field performance or formal accessibility audit was conducted.

    Change: Add a skip link, a meaningful H1, a main landmark, visible focus states, labeled controls, and header-aware anchor offsets. Defer the map until needed, lazy-load below-fold imagery, size images explicitly, and measure before/after performance on a real phone.

What I would keep.

  • The Mini-Cassia name and Lucky mascot.
  • The welcoming 5K run/walk positioning.
  • Real race photography and the 2025 participant record.
  • The established RaceEntry registration and Webscorer results destinations.
  • Local sponsor recognition, volunteering, and practical race information.

What I would not invent.

  • A new charitable beneficiary or fundraising total.
  • Testimonials, sponsor commitments, or race features.
  • Dog, stroller, or wheeled-device policies not confirmed in the current information.
  • Shirt guarantees, registration capacity, or additional discounts.
  • A fake course map that could be mistaken for the official route.
