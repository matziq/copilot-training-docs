    IntroA quick introduction and how to use this guide.

    Simple prompting

    Everything happens in Copilot Chat

    A prompt is simply an instruction you type. Copilot uses a large language model (LLM) to generate a response from patterns learned during training and the context available to it. A fluent answer can still be wrong; check it against your source.

    Each demo supplies its notes, starting prompt, follow-up prompts, and an answer checklist. You do not need to configure Outlook, Teams, polls, agents, memory, files, or a map service. Nothing is sent, posted, or scheduled. Use only the supplied practice text; no company-system access is needed for the scenarios.

    Open Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat and sign in with your work account. If chat is unavailable, contact your organization for access; do not enter work information into a personal account. Open any demo and follow A, B, C, then D. E, F, and G are optional tips and references. You can complete each demo independently.

    Use a new chat for each demo so notes do not get mixed together. If Copy is blocked, select the text and press Ctrl+C, then paste with Ctrl+V. If Copilot gives a longer acknowledgment, continue with B. No file upload or special command is required.

After you receive this source, reply only: "Got it. I'll use this as the source."
    B. Starting prompt (GCSE)▼
    Starting prompt - Goal, Context, Source, Expectation
    What this prompt doesTurn the supplied store-visit notes into useful follow-up text. You do not need a real mailbox, contact, or calendar event.
    Goal: Draft an email to Casey, a short action list, and reminder text for the district review.
Context: I am Jordan Reyes, the District Manager in these notes. Use a supportive tone and keep the email to 100 words or fewer. Return text here in chat; do not send or schedule anything.
Source: Use only the notes I pasted. Do not look up my email or calendar. Mark missing facts as not provided.
Expectation: Give me three headings: Email draft, Action list, and Reminder text. Include each stated owner and date, plus the November 24 review time. Do not invent causes or claim any action is complete.
    C. Follow-ups (2)▼
    Follow-ups - same thread
    What this prompt doesMake the email shorter in the same conversation.
    Shorten only the email to 60 words or fewer. Keep Casey's November 20 due date and the November 24 review. Leave the action list and reminder unchanged.
    What this prompt doesAsk Copilot to find and correct unsupported details.
    Compare the draft with my pasted notes. Check every name, count, date, and time. Correct anything unsupported, then show the final three parts.
    D. What to check▼
    • The email is for Casey and contains no more than 100 words (60 after the first follow-up).
    • The counts stay 4 of 7 shelf-label checks and about 7 minutes in the checkout line; no cause is invented.
    • Casey confirms the replacement by November 20. Jordan reviews the update November 24, 2:00-2:30 PM.
    • The result is text in chat. You do not need to create an Outlook item to finish.
    E. Tip: Text first, apps later▼

    An email draft and reminder wording are useful chat outputs. Sending email or adding a reminder is outside this demo; you can finish without knowing Outlook menus.

    Prompting and safetyClear instructions; evidence before action.

    Core habits

    Use the GCSE pattern

    State your Goal, give the relevant Context, identify the Source to use, and describe the output you Expect. Keep those instructions together, then refine the result in follow-up prompts.

    • Use a supplied source for specific facts; say when Copilot must not use outside information.
    • Check names, dates, counts, owners, recipients, and quoted language against the source.
    • Ask for uncertainty or missing details to be labeled. Never let a polished tone substitute for evidence.
    • Review audience and sensitivity before moving text from a private draft to a shared channel.
    • Confirm all actions yourself. A chat draft does not send an email, publish a post, create a poll, or schedule an event.

    For real work, follow Dollar General’s approved policies and your administrator’s instructions. This guide does not establish company policy.

    Copilot tips & tricksUseful habits and exercise shortcuts.

    Quick reference
    Ask any clarifying questions before responding▼

    Use this when missing audience, scope, source, format, date, or decision context could materially change the answer. Copilot can pause and request needed details instead of silently filling gaps. That may reduce assumptions and rework, but it does not replace source verification or human review.

    Keep channel posts shorter than private follow-ups▼

    Put the purpose and requested response near the top. Remove individual store details from a broad channel post unless sharing is authorized and necessary. Practice the Teams post and poll.

    Separate drafting from doing▼

    Ask for a draft, list, or proposed reminder; verify recipients and dates, then decide whether to use the result. Practice the Outlook follow-up.

    Check every fact against the source▼

    Recheck counts, boundaries, dates, owners, and qualifiers such as “about.” Ask Copilot to list unsupported statements, but make the final verification yourself. Practice the integrated workflow.
