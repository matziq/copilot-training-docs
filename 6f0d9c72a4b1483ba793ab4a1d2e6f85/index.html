Foundations for leaders
Access and safety
  • Work account. Sign in with your ELC work account. Enterprise data protection applies.
  • Permission-trimmed. Copilot sees only what you can already open.
  • Not used for training. Your prompts and data do not train foundation models.
  • You decide. Copilot drafts and recommends. Nothing is sent without you.
GCSE: how to ask
  • Goal: what you want, and the decision it supports.
  • Context: why you need it, who it's for, constraints.
  • Source: which emails, meetings, files, or web to use.
  • Expectation: format, length, and tone.
Working faster
  • Pick the model: Quick response for everyday work, Think deeper for strategy and tradeoffs, or Auto.
  • Type / to point at a file, meeting, email, or person. Type @ to call an agent.
  • Rename chats with a topic and date; save prompts you reuse.
  • Ask, sharpen, challenge ("What is fact, and what is inference?"), transform, act.
Do / Don't
  • Do ground prompts in your own authorized content and verify numbers against cited sources.
  • Do keep a human accountable for every output.
  • Don't ask Copilot to rate or judge individual people.
  • Don't forward AI output without reading it, or treat a coaching hypothesis as fact.

You have limited time to prepare, so spend it on the conversations with the most at stake. This prompt shows you where preparation will matter most, even when you're away from your desk.

GoalKnow which three conversations this week need the most preparation.
ContextThis week, between meetings.
SourceMy calendar plus related agendas, documents, and prior decisions.
ExpectationWhat's at stake, and what to know, prepare, decide, or bring.

Setup: Use your work account in the Copilot mobile app. In the session, type the prompt. Use voice later, in private.

Fast lane

Use this in the session.

Which three meetings this week need the most preparation from me, and why? For each, tell me what to know, decide, or bring. If an agenda or document is missing, say so and tell me what to ask my EA for. Keep it under 90 seconds to read.
"Agenda missing" is a finding, not a failure. Executive invites are often short placeholders. If Copilot flags missing agendas, that's your list of what to ask your EA to attach before the meeting. Add "attach the agenda and the pre-read" to your EA's routine, and this briefing gets better every week.
Full prompt and follow-ups
Full prompt: GCSE

Makes the request concrete enough to produce a short, useful preparation plan.

Goal: Identify the three upcoming conversations that need the most preparation.
Context: Use this week's calendar: [Date range and time zone]. My priorities are [Priorities].
Source: Use the upcoming meetings and authorized agendas, documents, and prior decisions available to you. Cite accessible items and say when an agenda or supporting document is missing.
Expectation: For each conversation, explain what's at stake and what I should know, prepare, decide, or bring. Recommend a preparation order based on decision urgency and missing information, not judgments about people. Where context is missing, list exactly what to request from my EA. Keep the overview under 90 seconds.

Voice is just a way to enter the request. It doesn't get around your account's permissions or change which features are available.

Follow-up 1: Focus on the next meeting
What should I know, prepare, decide, or bring before my next meeting? Give me the two most consequential preparation steps in under 45 seconds.
Follow-up 2: Keep the evidence handy
Now show a written checklist with the source links for that meeting, the decision I may need to make, and one question to resolve any missing context. Do not change my calendar.

Written links let you check a spoken summary before the meeting.

Run this on the presenter tenant first, using the fictional calendar, which has full agendas and pre-reads, so the room sees what a good result looks like. Then leaders run it on their own calendars. Ask: "Who got 'agenda missing'?" Use the show of hands to lead into the EA point.

Workflow 4 · Presenter showsMissed-meeting catch-upCopilot Chat / Teams recap

Missing a meeting shouldn't mean missing the context you need to lead. This prompt rebuilds the discussion, separates decisions from proposals, and shows where your input is needed, so you can step back in without slowing the team.

GoalAn executive summary of a meeting I missed.
ContextI need to re-engage without reopening settled decisions.
SourceThe transcript or recap, plus the files, threads, and chat I reference.
ExpectationWhat happened, decisions vs. proposals, what needs my input, and an action table.
This depends on transcription. Catch-up needs a meeting transcript or recap. In some European markets, transcription is limited by local policy or works-council agreements. If your meetings aren't transcribed, you can still paste approved notes, or ask your EA whether recap is enabled for the meetings you chair.

Setup: Choose one meeting that has a transcript or recap you can access. In Chat, type / to reference it. Otherwise, attach an authorized recap or paste approved notes. In Teams, open the past meeting and select Recap.

Fast lane
Catch me up on the [meeting name] meeting I missed: what was decided versus only proposed, what needs my input, and the actions with owners. Cite your sources.
Full prompt and follow-ups
Full prompt: GCSE
Goal: Prepare an executive summary of the meeting I missed.
Context: The meeting is [Meeting title] on [Date]. My role in the decision is [Role]. I need to reengage without reopening settled decisions.
Source: Use the selected meeting transcript or recap and the supporting documents, email threads, and meeting chat I explicitly reference. Cite the supporting source for each material point. State which sources were actually available; do not invent missing discussion.
Expectation: Give me: what happened; essential context; decisions made; outstanding decisions; and what needs my input. Separate proposals from agreed decisions. End with an action table: action, owner, due date, source. Write 'not stated' for missing owners or dates.

The answer depends on what meeting content exists and what you can access. A calendar entry alone isn't a transcript.

Follow-up 1: Verify the decisions
For each decision you listed, show the supporting quote or passage and source. Flag anything that was only a proposal or remains ambiguous, and correct the summary accordingly.
Follow-up 2: Prepare my reentry
Reduce this to a 60-second briefing and the three questions I should ask next. Prioritize questions that unblock a decision; do not repeat questions the meeting already resolved.

Show this pre-run on a fictional transcript, such as the Aurora decision meeting. Ask the room: "Whose leadership meetings are transcribed?" If it's under half, don't open it up for everyone to try. Point people to it as a take-home.

Fast lane
Give me a one-page briefing on my last seven days: outcomes, not activity. Cover key decisions, open commitments, and next week's priorities in under 500 words, with sources. Don't invent metrics.
Full prompt and follow-ups
Full prompt: GCSE
Goal: Create a one-page executive briefing focused on outcomes, not activity.
Context: Review [Start date] through [End date]. My current priorities are [Priorities]. The audience is me, preparing for next week.
Source: Use my authorized emails, meetings, chats, and documents available in this session for that period. Cite evidence. Distinguish my documented contribution from team outcomes; do not infer business value or completed work from attendance alone.

An email that says something is "planned" doesn't prove it was delivered.

Follow-up 2: Make it ready to schedule (after the session)

Pair exercise: Spar with Copilot

Pairs · Microsoft 365 Copilot mobile app, voice

Your second answer is always better than your first. Rehearse a tough conversation with a counterpart who pushes back, before it really matters.

Copilot plays the counterpart, pushes back, then critiques you. Swap roles. Finish by telling your partner one thing Copilot caught that you didn't.
Use a role, not a name. Say "a large retail partner" rather than naming the account, and keep figures and deal terms out of it. If you'd rather not use a real situation, take the fictional one below. Typing works too.
Your situation

Or use a fictional situation
