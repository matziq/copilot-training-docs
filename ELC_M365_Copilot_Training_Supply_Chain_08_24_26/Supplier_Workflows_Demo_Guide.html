Beginner prompt: explain the situation+

What this prompt does: Gives you a simple overview of what happened, what Orion is asking Aurelia to do, why it matters, and what information is still missing.

Goal: Help me understand the Aurelia-Orion supplier situation in plain language.
Context: I am new to this scenario and want a clear starting point before I analyze details or make a recommendation.
Source: Use only Demo_01_Aurelia_Supplier_Disruption_Brief.docx, Demo_02_Orion_Commercial_Adjustment_Proposal.docx, and the Exposure and Data_Dictionary sheets in Demo_04_Aurelia_Supplier_Exposure_and_Negotiation.xlsx.
Expectation: Break the answer into five short sections: (1) WHAT IS HAPPENING, (2) WHAT THE SUPPLIER IS ASKING FOR, (3) WHY IT MATTERS TO AURELIA, (4) WHAT WE KNOW, and (5) WHAT WE STILL NEED TO FIND OUT. Use bullets and plain language. Cite the source file or workbook sheet for important facts and numbers. Do not guess or add outside information.
Beginner follow-up: separate facts from claims+

What this prompt does: Sorts the information into confirmed facts, things the supplier says are true, and assumptions that still need to be checked.

Continue in the same thread. Create a simple table with three columns: CONFIRMED FACT, SUPPLIER CLAIM, and NEEDS VERIFICATION. Put each important point from the situation into the right column. For every supplier claim or item that needs verification, explain in one sentence what evidence would confirm it. Keep the language simple and cite the source.
Beginner follow-up: explain what to do next+

What this prompt does: Turns the overview into a short, practical next-step list so you know what to review, what to ask Orion, and what not to decide yet.

Based on the overview and fact-versus-claim table, give me the five most important next steps. For each step, show: ACTION, WHY IT MATTERS, WHO SHOULD HELP, and WHAT EVIDENCE IS NEEDED. Finish with three simple questions I should ask Orion before Aurelia considers the requested price change. Do not recommend approving or rejecting the request yet.

Goal: Prepare a negotiation control tower for the August 27 Orion meeting.

Expectation: Produce (1) objectives and interests for both parties, (2) BATNA and likely supplier BATNA, (3) target and walk-away by issue, (4) seven diagnostic questions, (5) a five-rung give-get concession ladder, and (6) an authority map showing what the category manager can decide versus what needs trade, legal, finance, supply-chain, or leadership approval. What this prompt does: Pressure-tests Aurelia's negotiation plan from Orion's point of view and reveals weak spots before the meeting.

Act as Orion's commercial director. Attack Aurelia's plan using the strongest plausible supplier arguments, identify where Aurelia is overconfident, and list the three questions most likely to put the category manager on the back foot.

Return to Aurelia's side. Write a 90-second opening statement that is firm, collaborative, and evidence-led. It should acknowledge legitimate disruption, reject blanket recovery, and propose a path to agreement around verified cost, launch protection, and two-way resets.

Current facts behind the fictional scenario

  • IMO reports continuing Red Sea security incidents; this does not mean Suez is officially closed.
  • UNCTAD reports that rerouting can lengthen journeys, skip port calls, create congestion, and raise costs. Use route-specific evidence.
  • Covered steel and aluminum products can face a 50% Section 232 duty, while enumerated Chinese goods can face a 25% Section 301 duty.
  • The temporary 10% Section 122 surcharge ended July 24, 2026 absent extension; Sections 232 and 301 remained unaffected.

The +10–14 day route delay, +32% ocean freight, supplier request, SKU exposure, and contract terms are fictional training assumptions—not current market quotations or ELC data. See the disruption brief for source URLs and exact dates.

Human review is part of the workflow

  • Customs and trade compliance owns HTS classification, origin, value, and duty applicability.
  • Legal counsel owns material contract interpretation, rights, remedies, and waiver risk.
  • Category managers own negotiation judgment within documented authority.
  • Use only approved Microsoft 365 locations and follow ELC sensitivity-label and information-protection requirements.
  • Verify every material number, source, assumption, and supplier commitment before use.

Make the workflow reusable

Verify → Challenge → Trade → Trigger. The bonus demo turns the work into an owned, reviewable GSM team resource instead of leaving it in one person's chat history.
