Read this before you study anything else

The ACT changed. If you practice with a book or website written before 2025, you will prepare for a test that no longer exists. The Enhanced ACT is shorter, Math now has four answer choices instead of five, and — the big one — Science no longer counts toward your composite score.

Your composite is now the average of English, Math, and Reading only. Everything on this site is built for the new format.

The one thing that changed everything

On the old ACT your Math score was one of four sections in the composite. On the Enhanced ACT it is one of three.

Your weakest section just became 33% of your score instead of 25%. Meanwhile Science — a 26 you would have had to defend — stopped counting at all.

Strategy in one line

Pour your time into Math. It is both your lowest score and the most coachable section on the test, because Math is the only section where the content is finite and memorizable.

In raw questions, this is smaller than it sounds

Math 24 → 28 is about 7 more correct questions out of 45. English 28 → 30 is about 3 more out of 50. Reading 26 → 28 is about 2 more out of 36. Twelve questions across the whole test. That is a realistic 26-day job, and it is why careless-error control matters as much as new content.

You have exactly three: Aug 29, Sep 5, Sep 12. Each one is a full-length, timed, phone-in-another-room dress rehearsal.

SUN

Sundays · the review

The day after each test you go through every wrong answer and write down the rule you broke. This is where the score actually moves.

Why the Sunday review matters more than the Saturday test

Taking a practice test measures your score. It does not raise it. The gain comes from the hour afterward, when you work out why each miss happened — a rule you never learned, a rule you know but rushed past, or a misread question. Sort every miss into one of those three buckets and the fix becomes obvious.

Test day logistics

Pack the night before

  • Printed admission ticket and a photo ID
  • An approved calculator with fresh batteries, plus a backup set
  • Several sharpened No. 2 pencils and a soft eraser — no mechanical pencils
  • An analog watch with no beeping, or nothing at all
  • Water and a real snack for the break
  • Layers — testing rooms are unpredictable

The morning of

  • Eat protein, not just sugar. Do not skip breakfast.
  • Arrive early enough that traffic cannot rattle you.
  • Warm your brain up on five easy Math questions in the car. Do not learn anything new.
  • If a question is taking too long, mark it, guess, and move. You can come back.
  • Fill in every bubble. There is no penalty for a wrong answer, ever.
  • A bad section is not a bad test. Reset at the break and keep going.
