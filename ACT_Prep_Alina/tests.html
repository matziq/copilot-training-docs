The Three Practice Tests

One for each Saturday you have left. Each is a full-length Enhanced ACT: 50 English, 45 Math, 36 Reading, and 40 Science questions, every section on its own clock.

Take these seriously or do not take them at all

A practice test taken with your phone nearby, in three sittings, with the timer paused, tells you nothing. Sit down at 9:00 am, put the phone in another room, use a real calculator and scratch paper, take one ten-minute break after Math, and do not stop. The score you get under real conditions is the only number worth having.

Three is the right number

More full tests would not help you. Full-length tests are expensive in time and energy, and they measure far more than they teach.

What actually raises a score is the day after the test, when you work out why each miss happened. Three tests give you three full review cycles, and each cycle points your weekday work at something specific.

On weekdays, use the topic drills and single timed sections instead. Same benefit, an hour instead of four.

Test day rules to rehearse

Timing

  • English — 50 questions, 35 minutes. Seven minutes per passage.
  • Math — 45 questions, 50 minutes. Be at question 30 by minute 30.
  • Break — ten minutes. Eat something.
  • Reading — 36 questions, 40 minutes. Ten minutes per passage.
  • Science — 40 questions, 40 minutes. Six to seven minutes per passage.

Rules

  • Never leave a bubble blank. Four choices now, so a guess is worth 25%.
  • If a question passes 90 seconds, guess, flag it, and move.
  • Do not go back to a section whose time has ended. You cannot on test day.
  • A bad section is not a bad test. Reset and keep going.
  • Do not review the test the same day you take it.

Practice a single section instead

On a weekday you will not have four hours. Run one section on its own clock — it still trains pacing, and it is the backbone of your weekday plan.
