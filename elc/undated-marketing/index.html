Start here

These exercises use fictional text pasted into chat. They do not depend on connected work data, file upload, agents, or actions. Use only the Copilot experience and work account approved by your organization; feature and data availability depend on license and tenant settings.

Do not paste real customer, employee, campaign, or confidential information. The practice cases are fictional. Treat every output as a draft for human review, not an approval, claim substantiation, or executed action.

01

Prepare a launch-readiness huddleSeparate the approved baseline from proposals and open evidence.

Chat · paste-in
What you'll practice · Who it's for▼
What it does

Turn a short launch case into a source-grounded marketing huddle brief without turning a proposal into a decision.

Who it's for: Alex Morgan, a fictional regional marketing team member preparing for a leadership review.
1. Paste the practice source▼
Paste this first - practice source
Open TXT
Practice persona: Alex Morgan, a fictional regional marketing team member.
Practice date: November 6, 2026.

Source A — Project Luminara leadership review invitation
Review date: November 9, 2026. Purpose: review readiness for a fictional seasonal skincare launch in the UK, Ireland, and the Nordics. Baseline target launch: November 23, 2026. Leadership must decide whether to keep the baseline or request a revised plan after readiness evidence is reviewed. The invitation alone does not authorize a launch-date change.

Source B — Project Luminara project update, November 2, 2026
Brand team: creative review due November 6; approval is not yet confirmed. Commercial team: UK retailer artwork due November 10. Supply team: stock-readiness confirmation due November 12; confirmation has not been received. Nordic localization: Norwegian packaging translation remains open; no owner or completion date is recorded in this update. Proposal: consider moving the launch to November 30 if localization cannot be resolved. This is a proposal, not an approved decision.

Source C — Project Luminara leadership note, November 2, 2026
The approved baseline remains November 23. No revised launch date has been approved. Bring creative approval, stock confirmation, and localization status to the November 9 review. A localization owner and due date still need to be agreed. Do not attribute a decision to an individual without evidence.
Starting prompt (GCSE)▼
Starting prompt - Goal, Context, Source, Expectation
What this prompt doesThis prompt asks Copilot to synthesize a small case while keeping the approved date, proposed date, and unresolved evidence distinct.
Goal: Help me prepare for the November 9 leadership review.
Context: I am Alex Morgan. I am preparing for the November 9, 2026 review of a fictional seasonal skincare launch.
Source: Use only the pasted Project Luminara case. Separate approved decisions, proposals, open items, and missing evidence.
Expectation: Give me the purpose, three key facts, open decisions, and two questions to ask. Cite the source for each fact and flag anything missing or uncertain.
Follow-ups (2)▼
Follow-ups - same thread
What this prompt doesA source check catches an attractive but unsupported conclusion before the brief is reused.
Goal: Verify the date and milestone status in the case.
Context: I am Alex Morgan preparing the November 9, 2026 leadership review.
Source: Use the same pasted Project Luminara case and the previous answer; do not add outside facts.
Expectation: List confirmed dates separately from proposals, show the supporting source for each, and do not treat a due date as proof of completion.
What this prompt doesThis adapts the verified brief for a live discussion without implying approval or execution.
Goal: Turn the verified case into a short speaking note.
Context: I am Alex Morgan preparing to speak at the November 9, 2026 leadership review.
Source: Use only the same pasted Project Luminara case and facts already checked in this chat.
Expectation: Write a 60-second speaking note that ends with the unresolved evidence and owner/date questions. Do not imply the launch date changed.
What to check▼
  • November 23 remains the approved baseline; November 30 is only a proposal.
  • Creative approval, stock confirmation, and Norwegian localization are still open or unconfirmed.
  • The source does not name a localization owner or completion date.
  • No decision or statement is attributed to a person without evidence.
Tip: Separate status from decision▼
Ask for separate labels such as approved, proposed, and open. A confident summary is not a new approval.
Did you know? The date boundary is explicit▼
In this fictional case, November 23 is the approved baseline, while November 30 is only a proposal. Both dates appear in the source, but they do not have the same status.
Recommended custom instructionKeep status summaries evidence-based▼

Optional: use this only if it matches your real work. Do not save fictional project details.

When summarizing project status, separate approved decisions, proposals, open items, and missing evidence. Do not infer owners or decisions.

02

Build an evidence-led audience hypothesisFind audience needs without inventing demographics or prevalence.

Chat · paste-in
What you'll practice · Who it's for▼
What it does

Practice turning a small, synthetic set of customer-style comments into clearly labeled segment hypotheses and research questions.

Who it's for: A fictional marketing researcher working with a practice-only qualitative snapshot.
1. Paste the practice source▼
Paste this first - practice source
Open TXT
Practice source: synthetic qualitative notes for keyboard practice only. These are not real customer comments, ELC research, or a representative sample. The file contains no demographic or market-size data.

Product context: a fictional evening skincare routine called Project Luminara. No product benefits, price, or launch date are established by these notes.

R01: "I want a short routine after work."
R02: "Tell me plainly what each product does; I do not want a complicated ritual."
R03: "I ignore claims like 'overnight transformation'; tell me what is actually in it."
R04: "I forget steps when I travel; a compact routine matters."
R05: "I am not looking for a new routine; a clear one-step addition feels manageable."
R06: "I want a gift that feels thoughtful, but I need to know what is in the box."
R07: "Refill options are interesting only if I can find refills where I shop."
R08: "I pay attention to scent; fragrance-free options are easier for me."

Limitations: eight fictional notes are not a population estimate, preference ranking, or proof of purchase intent. Do not infer age, gender, income, ethnicity, skin condition, location, or other demographics.
Starting prompt (GCSE)▼
Starting prompt - Goal, Context, Source, Expectation
What this prompt doesThis adapts a marketing persona-building exercise into an evidence-led task: describe needs, not imagined people.
Goal: Create provisional audience need-state hypotheses from the practice notes.
Context: I am a fictional marketing researcher exploring messages for a fictional evening skincare routine.
Source: Use only the pasted synthetic notes. Treat each note as a fictional quote; do not infer demographics, market size, or purchase intent.
Expectation: Suggest up to three need-state hypotheses in a table with a neutral label, supporting participant IDs and exact phrases, one message hypothesis, and one uncertainty to validate. Mark each hypothesis as unvalidated.
Follow-ups (2)▼
Follow-ups - same thread
What this prompt doesThis asks Copilot to challenge its own framing instead of making a polished guess sound like research.
Goal: Stress-test the audience labels and message hypotheses.
Context: I am a fictional marketing researcher reviewing provisional audience hypotheses.
Source: Use only the same pasted synthetic notes and the hypotheses in the previous answer.
Expectation: Identify any stereotype, unsupported demographic assumption, or claim beyond the notes, then rewrite the affected labels and hypotheses neutrally.
What this prompt doesA useful hypothesis points to a practical validation step, not a budget decision.
Goal: Create interview questions to test the strongest audience hypothesis.
Context: I am a fictional marketing researcher planning a follow-up interview.
Source: Use only the same pasted synthetic notes and the strongest hypothesis from the previous answer.
Expectation: Write three open-ended questions. For each, describe an answer that would support the hypothesis and one that would weaken it.
What to check▼
  • Every need-state is tied to participant IDs and exact words in the source.
  • Labels describe a possible need, not a real person or demographic segment.
  • No prevalence, market size, purchase intent, or ranking is invented.
  • All results are called hypotheses that need validation.
Tip: Keep the evidence column▼
Ask for participant IDs and exact phrases beside each insight. If a statement has no evidence cell, treat it as a question to test rather than a finding.
Did you know? Eight notes are not a market estimate▼
The source contains eight synthetic notes and no representative-sampling or demographic data. They can support practice hypotheses, not a claim about how common a preference is.
Recommended custom instructionLabel audience evidence and hypotheses▼

Optional: keep this as a work-style preference, not as stored customer research.

When developing audience segments, distinguish source evidence from hypotheses. Do not infer demographic traits or prevalence when the source does not provide them.

03

Adapt a launch idea across channelsCreate channel-ready drafts while protecting product facts.

Chat · paste-in
What you'll practice · Who it's for▼
What it does

Practice the multi-channel adaptation pattern from the marketing prompt guide using a fully fictional brief and explicit claim limits.

Who it's for: A fictional ELC marketing team member drafting practice copy for an invented product.
1. Paste the practice source▼
Paste this first - practice source
Open TXT
Practice source: fictional product brief for keyboard practice only. It is not an ELC product or approved marketing copy.

Product name: Luminara Evening Ritual.
Product format: a fictional three-piece skincare set: cleanser, moisturizer, and face oil.
Approved practice facts: the set is intended for an evening routine. The fictional moisturizer ingredient list includes glycerin and squalane.
Audience: not specified.
Launch date and availability: not approved or supplied.
Price: not supplied.
Approved efficacy, clinical, environmental, or sourcing claims: none supplied.
Brand voice: calm, clear, and sensory without exaggerating.

Do not claim a clinical result, treatment, transformation, sustainability benefit, or launch availability. Do not invent a quote, price, ingredient, testimonial, or proof point. The output is a draft for review, not approved advertising.
Starting prompt (GCSE)▼
Starting prompt - Goal, Context, Source, Expectation
What this prompt doesThis reuses the multi-channel marketing prompt pattern while making the factual boundary and output limits explicit.
Goal: Draft a small set of channel-specific launch messages for a fictional skincare set.
Context: I am a fictional marketing writer. The audience and launch date are not supplied, so keep the copy general and flag what needs approval.
Source: Use only the pasted practice brief for product facts. Do not invent claims, price, availability, testimonials, or audience details.
Expectation: Write an Instagram caption of no more than 40 words, a LinkedIn post of no more than 55 words, and an email subject line of no more than 40 characters plus preview text of no more than 45 characters. Add factual details that need confirmation before publication.
Follow-ups (2)▼
Follow-ups - same thread
What this prompt doesA claim-by-claim audit makes it easier to spot invented benefits or availability.
Goal: Audit the draft for unsupported product claims.
Context: I am a fictional marketing writer checking a draft before human review.
Source: Use only the same pasted fictional product brief and the draft in the previous answer.
Expectation: For each factual statement, cite the exact supporting line. Remove or flag unsupported content and report each item's word or character count.
What this prompt doesThis varies the creative without relaxing the source boundary.
Goal: Create two alternate tones for the channel copy.
Context: I am a fictional marketing writer refining draft channel copy.
Source: Use only the same pasted fictional product brief and the copy in the previous answer.
Expectation: Provide one editorial and one concise alternative. Preserve the factual and channel limits and do not add a launch date.
What to check▼
  • The set contains only the cleanser, moisturizer, and face oil named in the brief.
  • Glycerin and squalane are attributed only to the fictional moisturizer ingredient list.
  • No efficacy, clinical, sustainability, sourcing, price, audience, or availability claims appear.
  • All three channel limits are checked and the result is still marked as a draft.
Tip: Treat limits as testable▼
Ask Copilot to report a word or character count, then verify the exact platform limit yourself. Keep each channel's count separate.
Did you know? A draft is not approval▼
The source supplies no approved efficacy claim, price, audience, or launch date. Leaving those details out is a correct result, not a gap to fill with a plausible-sounding guess.
Recommended custom instructionDraft with substantiation boundaries▼

Optional: use this only for your own recurring drafting preferences; follow your organization's review process.

For marketing drafts, use only the product facts I provide, flag claims that need substantiation, and preserve requested channel limits. Mark drafts as drafts.

04

Read a campaign performance snapshotCheck arithmetic and keep attributed ROAS in context.

Chat · paste-in
What you'll practice · Who it's for▼
What it does

Calculate a small campaign snapshot by hand-in-the-loop prompting, then decide what the data can and cannot support.

Who it's for: A fictional marketing analyst reviewing synthetic pilot results.
1. Paste the practice source▼
Paste this first - practice source
Open TXT
Practice source: synthetic campaign data for keyboard practice only. Currency: USD. Reporting period: October 1-31, 2026.

Campaign | Spend | Platform-attributed revenue | Attributed orders
Email - Evening ritual | 1200 | 7800 | 156
Paid social - Evening ritual | 3600 | 12600 | 210
Search - Branded routine | 2400 | 10800 | 180
Creator sampling | 1800 | 4500 | 90

Definitions and limitations:
ROAS = platform-attributed revenue divided by spend.
The revenue values use last-click attribution and are not an estimate of incremental revenue.
Production costs, product margin, returns, audience overlap, and customer acquisition cost are not provided.
The data is fictional; do not recommend a real budget change from it.
Starting prompt (GCSE)▼
Starting prompt - Goal, Context, Source, Expectation
What this prompt doesThis turns a performance-analysis prompt into a checkable calculation instead of an unsupported budget recommendation.
Goal: Summarize the synthetic campaign results and calculate ROAS transparently.
Context: I am a fictional marketing analyst preparing a practice-only readout for October 1-31, 2026.
Source: Use only the pasted table and definitions. Treat all revenue as platform-attributed, last-click, and non-incremental.
Expectation: Show total spend, total attributed revenue, total orders, blended ROAS, and each campaign's ROAS to two decimal places. Show the formula, identify the leader by ROAS and by revenue separately, and state what the data cannot prove.
Follow-ups (2)▼
Follow-ups - same thread
What this prompt doesA second calculation makes the underlying arithmetic visible and catches metric mix-ups.
Goal: Recalculate the campaign totals and ROAS.
Context: I am a fictional marketing analyst checking a practice readout.
Source: Use only the same pasted campaign table, definitions, and the previous answer.
Expectation: Show the arithmetic for every total and ROAS, identify any mismatch with the first answer, and distinguish the revenue leader from the ROAS leader.
What this prompt doesA next step can be useful without pretending that last-click revenue proves incremental impact.
Goal: Suggest careful next analyses or experiments.
Context: I am a fictional marketing analyst planning what to investigate next.
Source: Use only the same pasted campaign table and definitions; do not treat attributed revenue as incremental impact.
Expectation: Suggest two low-risk analyses or experiments and name the additional data each needs. Do not recommend a budget shift or claim causality.
What to check▼
  • Total spend is $9,000; attributed revenue is $35,700; attributed orders are 636.
  • Blended ROAS is $35,700 / $9,000 = 3.97x.
  • Email has the highest ROAS at 6.50x; paid social has the highest attributed revenue at $12,600.
  • Creator sampling has the lowest ROAS at 2.50x, but margin, incrementality, returns, and audience overlap are unknown.
  • No real budget move is justified by this fictional last-click snapshot alone.
Tip: Ask for formulas, not just rankings▼
Specify the numerator, denominator, rounding, and whether you want the largest value or best ratio. Those are different questions.
Did you know? Revenue is not incrementality▼
The source defines $35,700 as platform-attributed revenue and says the attribution is last-click, not incremental. That is why a 3.97x blended ROAS is not proof of profit or causal lift.
Recommended custom instructionMake metric math auditable▼

Optional: use this for future analysis preferences, not as a saved copy of this fictional dataset.

When analyzing marketing metrics, show the formula and arithmetic, check totals, and distinguish attributed results from incremental impact.

Prompting and safetyClear instructions; evidence before action.

Core habits

Use GCSE to make a prompt easy to check: Goal (what you need), Context (who you are and how you will use it), Source (what evidence to use), and Expectation (format, audience, length, or limits). Microsoft Learn teaches the same four elements for effective Copilot prompts.

A quick source-check routine
  1. Ask for source phrases beside facts or calculations.
  2. Check dates, names, figures, limits, and decision status against the pasted source.
  3. Ask Copilot to mark missing evidence instead of filling the gap.
  4. Review any draft before using it; a chat response does not approve or send anything.

Keep to your organization's data-handling rules. Copilot features and available data sources vary by license and tenant settings.

Copilot tips & tricksUseful habits and exercise shortcuts.

Quick reference
Keep the source visible▼
Paste the complete fictional source before the starting prompt. Use the readiness case to practice separating a decision from a proposal.
Iterate without losing the boundary▼
Use follow-ups to audit, shorten, or adapt an answer. Keep the same source and factual limits in the thread; see the channel adaptation exercise.
Ask for evidence beside the idea▼
Pair each audience hypothesis with participant IDs and exact phrases in the research exercise. A plausible marketing insight is still a hypothesis until validated.
Check the math yourself▼
For metrics, specify the formula and show the arithmetic. In the campaign snapshot, revenue and ROAS answer different questions.
