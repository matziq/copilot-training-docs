    Analyze a campaign in ExcelTurn a raw KPI table into clear, checked insights.

    Copilot in Excel
    A. Paste the practice source▼

    Practice workbook: Download ELC_DACH_Velvet_Frost_Campaign_Practice.xlsx. Save it to your OneDrive, open it in Excel, and select the Copilot icon. No Copilot in Excel? Paste the source below into Copilot Chat instead.

    B. Starting prompt (GCSE)▼
    Starting prompt - Goal, Context, Source, Expectation
    What this prompt doesAsks Copilot to calculate the standard media KPIs, compare them with the targets, and explain the result in plain language.
    Goal: Analyze the Velvet Frost campaign results and tell me which channels and markets meet the ROAS and CPA targets.
Context: I am preparing a performance review for the DACH marketing leadership team, so I need a clear view of what worked and what did not.
Source: Use only the campaign data, targets, KPI definitions, and caveats in this workbook or the pasted source.
Expectation: Give me a table with ROAS and CPA by channel, the campaign total, and three plain-language insights. Do not speculate about brand impact.
    C. Follow-ups (2)▼
    Follow-ups - same thread
    What this prompt doesMoves from a total to the row-level detail and asks for the outliers.
    Which market and channel combination has the lowest ROAS and which has the highest? Show the numbers you used.
    What this prompt doesAsks for a chart you could reuse in the story deck.
    Create a chart that compares ROAS by channel against the 3.0 target.
    D. What to check▼
    • Total: spend EUR 260,000, revenue EUR 750,550, ROAS 2.89 (misses the 3.0 target), CPA EUR 38.35 (meets the EUR 40 target), 6,780 conversions.
    • Paid Social: spend EUR 80,000, revenue EUR 218,650, ROAS 2.73 (misses 3.0), CPA EUR 39.22 (meets EUR 40), CTR 0.84%.
    • Paid Search: spend EUR 62,000, revenue EUR 277,000, ROAS 4.47 (meets 3.0), CPA EUR 24.03 (meets EUR 40), CTR 5.11%.
    • Online Video: spend EUR 70,000, revenue EUR 72,700, ROAS 1.04 (misses 3.0), CPA EUR 107.69 (misses EUR 40), CTR 0.30%.
    • Retail Media: spend EUR 48,000, revenue EUR 182,200, ROAS 3.80 (meets 3.0), CPA EUR 31.79 (meets EUR 40), CTR 1.20%.
    • Lowest row: CH Online Video, ROAS 0.90. Highest row: CH Paid Search, ROAS 4.68.
    • Copilot should not claim that Online Video built awareness or brand lift; the source says that study is not available yet.
    E. Tip: Format the data as a table▼

    Copilot in Excel works best with a clean range: one header row, no blank rows, and consistent data types. The practice workbook already formats the data as an Excel table named CampaignData. Save it to OneDrive or SharePoint so Copilot can work with it. If you do not see Copilot in Excel, use the Copilot Chat fallback: paste the source and the same prompt.

    F. Did you know? Copilot in Excel can also make PivotTables▼

    Besides formulas and charts, Copilot in Excel can create PivotTables, highlight, sort, and filter data, and import data from other workbooks. Get started with Copilot in Excel

    G. Recommended custom instructionShow your calculations▼

    A stable, nonsensitive working preference.

    When you calculate numbers for me, show the formula or the inputs you used so I can check them.

    Add a KPI block to the existing workbookExtend the workbook with formulas, not retyped numbers.

    Copilot in Excel
    A. Paste the practice source▼
    Paste this first - practice source
    Expected acknowledgmentAfter you paste the source, Copilot should reply only: “Got it. I'll use this as the source.”
    Open TXT
    FICTIONAL TRAINING SCENARIO: VELVET FROST KPI BLOCK REQUEST
Requested by: Jonas Brunner, Brand Manager, Austria (fictional)
Date: Tuesday, November 10, 2026
Workbook: ELC_DACH_Velvet_Frost_Campaign_Practice.xlsx, table CampaignData (same data as exercise 01)
Request: add a market summary block on a new sheet named KPI_Summary.
Rows: DE, AT, CH, then a Total row.
Columns: Market, Spend_EUR, Revenue_EUR, Conversions, ROAS, CPA_EUR, ROAS_Status
ROAS_Status rule: "Meets" when ROAS is at least 3.0; otherwise "Below".
Also add the same four KPIs by channel as a second block below the market block.
Rules: use formulas that reference the CampaignData table. Do not type numbers by hand.
Formatting: ROAS with two decimals, CPA as EUR with two decimals, and highlight "Below" in light red.
Caveat: Revenue is attributed online revenue (last click, 7-day window).
After you receive this source, reply only: "Got it. I'll use this as the source."
    B. Starting prompt (GCSE)▼
    Starting prompt - Goal, Context, Source, Expectation
    What this prompt doesAsks Copilot to build a structured summary block with live formulas and a target status flag.
    Goal: Add a KPI summary block to this workbook as described in the request.
Context: The block will be reused every week, so it must recalculate when the CampaignData table changes.
Source: Use the CampaignData table and the block request only.
Expectation: Create the KPI_Summary sheet with formulas, the status rule, and the requested formatting, then tell me which cells you added.
    C. Follow-ups (2)▼
    Follow-ups - same thread
    What this prompt doesChecks the block against the raw data, which is the fastest way to catch a wrong range.
    Check that the Total row equals the sum of the CampaignData table for spend, revenue, and conversions. Tell me if anything does not match.
    What this prompt doesAdds a simple what-if view without changing the source data.
    Add one more row that shows ROAS for all channels except Online Video, and label it as a what-if view.
    D. What to check▼
    • DE: spend EUR 156,000, revenue EUR 453,300, ROAS 2.91, CPA EUR 37.50.
    • AT: spend EUR 45,000, revenue EUR 128,050, ROAS 2.85, CPA EUR 38.14.
    • CH: spend EUR 59,000, revenue EUR 169,200, ROAS 2.87, CPA EUR 40.97.
    • The block's total row must equal the raw data: EUR 260,000 spend and EUR 750,550 revenue. If it does not, a formula range is wrong.
    • Status column: all three markets show "Below" (each is under 3.0). In the channel block, only Paid Search and Retail Media show "Meets".
    • Without Online Video, the other three channels return ROAS 3.57. This is a what-if view, not a forecast.
    • Click into a few cells and read the formulas. Confirm that Copilot added formulas rather than typed-in numbers.
    E. Tip: Ask for formulas, then inspect them▼

    Say "use formulas" so the block recalculates when data changes. Then click a few cells and read the formula bar. A total that does not match the raw data usually means a range or filter is wrong. In Copilot Chat, ask for the formulas as text and paste them yourself.

    F. Did you know? Copilot opens from the lower right▼

    In current Excel versions, you open Copilot from the Copilot icon in the lower right of the window. If you do not see it, your subscription or organization settings may not include it. Microsoft Support: Copilot in Excel

    G. Recommended memoryPreferred KPI formats▼

    Safe to save: a formatting preference, not campaign data.

    Remember that I format ROAS with two decimals and money as EUR with two decimals.

    FICTIONAL TRAINING SCENARIO: DACH MARKETING HUB SITE, RESOURCE LIST
Site: DACH Marketing Hub (fictional SharePoint site)
Exported by: Sophie Keller, Marketing Operations, Switzerland (fictional)
Exported on: Tuesday, November 10, 2026
R1 | FY27 Media KPI Definitions and Targets | Owner: Lena Hofmann | Updated: Monday, September 7, 2026 | Status: Approved | ROAS target 3.0, CPA target EUR 40
R2 | FY25 DACH Media Benchmarks deck | Owner: former agency team | Updated: Friday, March 15, 2024 | Status: Archived | ROAS target 2.5
R3 | Velvet Frost Brand Messaging Guide | Owner: Brand Team DACH | Updated: Thursday, August 20, 2026 | Status: Approved | Key message: "Frosted color, velvet comfort"
R4 | Agency Recap Velvet Frost Week 3 | Owner: Media agency (fictional) | Updated: Friday, October 23, 2026 | Status: Final for week 3 only | Covers October 5 - October 25, 2026
R5 | DACH Media Plan Q4 2026 | Owner: not listed | Updated: Wednesday, July 1, 2026 | Status: Unknown | Planned budget EUR 250,000
R6 | Velvet Frost Creative Test Results | Owner: Jonas Brunner | Updated: Monday, November 9, 2026 | Status: Draft, not reviewed | Claims video drove brand lift
R7 | Retail Store Training Calendar 2027 | Owner: Retail Education | Updated: Monday, November 2, 2026 | Status: Approved | Store staff training dates
After you receive this source, reply only: "Got it. I'll use this as the source."
    B. Starting prompt (GCSE)▼
    Starting prompt - Goal, Context, Source, Expectation
    What this prompt doesAsks Copilot to triage the resources by trust and relevance, and invites it to ask questions first.
    Goal: Assess which resources I should use to build a Velvet Frost campaign results story.
Context: The story goes to DACH marketing leadership, so I need current, approved sources and I must avoid outdated targets.
Source: Use only the resource list from the DACH Marketing Hub site.
Expectation: Sort each resource into Use, Check first, Do not use, or Not relevant, with one reason based on owner, date, status, or conflict. Ask any clarifying questions before responding.
    C. Follow-ups (2)▼
    Follow-ups - same thread
    What this prompt doesSurfaces conflicts that could put a wrong number into the story.
    Do any of these resources conflict with each other? Name the conflict and which source should win.
    What this prompt doesTurns the review into an action you can send, without sending it.
    Draft a short message to Sophie Keller asking who owns the DACH Media Plan Q4 2026 and whether it is current. Do not send anything.
    D. What to check▼
    • Use: R1 (FY27 KPI definitions, current, owner Lena Hofmann) and R3 (Velvet Frost brand messaging, approved).
    • Do not use: R2 (FY25 benchmark deck with a ROAS target of 2.5, superseded by R1's 3.0) and R6 (draft creative test results, not reviewed).
    • Check first: R4 (agency recap, last updated before the flight ended) and R5 (DACH media plan, no owner listed).
    • Not relevant: R7 (retail store training calendar).
    • Copilot should point out the conflict between ROAS 2.5 and ROAS 3.0, and should not treat a draft as approved.
    E. Tip: Owner, date, status, conflict▼

    For every source, check four things: who owns it, when it was updated, whether it is approved, and whether it conflicts with another source. On a real site, Copilot in SharePoint can summarize and compare files you can access. Always open the file before you reuse a number from it.

    F. Did you know? Copilot in SharePoint is rolling out▼

    Copilot in SharePoint requires a Microsoft 365 Copilot license. General availability rollout started on September 30, 2026. Everyday capabilities are included, while some advanced work uses Copilot Credits. Get started with Copilot in SharePoint

    G. Recommended custom instructionFlag outdated sources▼

    A stable, nonsensitive working method.

    When you use documents, tell me each document's last updated date and flag anything older than 12 months or marked as draft.

After you receive this source, reply only: "Got it. I'll use this as the source."
    B. Starting prompt (GCSE)▼
    Starting prompt - Goal, Context, Source, Expectation
    What this prompt doesGives Copilot in PowerPoint the audience, storyline, and checked numbers so the draft starts close to final.
    Goal: Create a five-slide presentation that tells the Velvet Frost DACH results story.
Context: I will present it to DACH marketing leadership in a 15-minute review, and they need a clear proposal to decide on.
Source: Use only the verified results, caveat, key message, and storyline in the story brief.
Expectation: Follow the five-slide storyline exactly, with one headline and no more than four bullets per slide. Add speaker notes. Do not invent quotes, images of people, or brand-lift claims.
    C. Follow-ups (2)▼
    Follow-ups - same thread
    What this prompt doesStrengthens the headlines so leadership gets the point from titles alone.
    Rewrite each slide title as a full-sentence takeaway, such as a headline a busy leader can read in five seconds.
    What this prompt doesTurns the results slide into a visual.
    On the results slide, add a simple chart or table comparing ROAS by channel with the 3.0 target.
    D. What to check▼
    • Exactly five slides, in the storyline order from the brief.
    • The numbers match the brief: ROAS 2.89 against a 3.0 target; Paid Search ROAS 4.47; Online Video ROAS 1.04.
    • No invented quotes, logos, images of real people, or brand-lift claims.
    • The recommendation is labeled a proposal for leadership to decide, not a decision.
    • The deck uses your organization's template or Brand Kit if available. Check fonts, colors, and logos before sharing.
    E. Tip: Use your brand, then review it▼

    When you create a presentation with Copilot, choose Select brand if your organization has a Brand Kit, or start from your corporate template. The DACH brand master was not available for this guide, so check fonts, colors, logos, and images against your brand rules before you share the deck.

    F. Did you know? Copilot in PowerPoint can start from a file▼

    Copilot in PowerPoint can create a presentation from a prompt or from a file you reference, and it can apply your organization's Brand Kit when one is available. Create a new presentation with Copilot in PowerPoint

    G. Recommended custom instructionExecutive slide style▼

    A stable, nonsensitive style preference.

    For leadership slides, use full-sentence takeaway titles, no more than four bullets per slide, and put details in the speaker notes.

    Prompting and safetyClear instructions; evidence before action.

    Core habits

    Copilot is built on large language models (LLMs). An LLM breaks text into small units called tokens and predicts likely text based on context. Grounding gives it the right source, such as your workbook or a document; a good prompt tells it what to do with that source. Microsoft prompt guidance · What are LLMs?

    Goal

    What do I want Copilot to do?

    Context

    Why do I need it, and who is the audience?

    Source

    Which data or files should Copilot use, and nothing else?

    Expectation

    What format, length, tone, and boundaries should the answer follow?

    Iterate instead of restarting. Stay in the same chat and ask Copilot to show its calculation, shorten, change a chart, list unknowns, or fix one weak GCSE line.
    Responsible use checklist▼
    • Sign in with your work account so enterprise data protection applies.
    • Share only the minimum information the task needs.
    • Recalculate or spot-check key numbers before they go into a deck.
    • Check that a source is current and approved before you reuse it.
    • Use only approved brand and product claims in anything external.
    • Review, edit, and own the final version. A draft never sends or approves anything.
    • Follow your company's policy and your tenant's controls; this guide does not replace them.

    Copilot tips & tricksUseful habits and exercise shortcuts.

    Quick reference
    Ask clarifying questions before Copilot answers▼

    Add “Ask any clarifying questions before responding.” when the answer depends on details you might have left out, such as audience, scope, source, format, or dates.

    This gives Copilot permission to pause, point out what is unclear, and ask for what it needs instead of silently filling gaps. It can reduce assumptions and rework, but it does not replace checking the source or reviewing the final output.

    Ask any clarifying questions before responding.
    Keep refining in the same chat▼

    Follow-ups build on what Copilot already knows from the conversation. Ask it to change a chart, add a column, or tighten a slide instead of starting over. Start a new chat when you switch to an unrelated topic so old details do not leak into new work.

    Keep the same numbers, but make this shorter and easier for a leadership audience to scan.
