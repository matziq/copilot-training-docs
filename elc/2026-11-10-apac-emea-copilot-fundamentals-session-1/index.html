    Which license does this guide assume?▼

    Every core exercise works with Microsoft Copilot Chat, which is available to work accounts and uses web data plus the content you paste, upload, or have open in Outlook and Teams. Notes marked Optional for Microsoft 365 Copilot license holders describe extras such as grounding in your mailbox, files, and meetings. If you are unsure which you have, follow the core steps. Features also depend on tenant settings, rollout, and policy.

    Safe practice in one minute▼
    • Use the fictional sources in this guide; do not paste confidential, personal, or customer data into practice chats.
    • Follow ELC policy for real work.
    • Check names, numbers, dates, and claims before you share anything.
    • Copilot drafts; you decide. Nothing in a chat sends, approves, or publishes on its own.

    01

    Your first prompt in Copilot ChatFind Copilot, paste a source, and get a grounded briefing.

    Copilot Chat
    What you'll practice · Who it's for▼
    What it does

    Open Microsoft Copilot Chat with your work account, give it one short source, and turn it into a briefing you could use on your first day on a project.

    Who it's for: Everyone, especially first-time Copilot users
    1. Paste the practice source▼
    Paste this first - practice source
    Expected acknowledgmentAfter you paste the source, Copilot should reply only: “Got it. I'll use this as the source.”
    Open TXT
    FICTIONAL TRAINING SCENARIO: PROJECT SOLSTICE
Prepared by: Mei Tanaka, Regional Education Lead, APAC (fictional)
Date: Tuesday, November 10, 2026
Product: Lumen Dew Hydrating Serum (fictional), 30 ml
Launch windows:
- APAC wave: Singapore, Sydney, Seoul - in store from Tuesday, January 12, 2027
- EMEA wave: London, Paris, Dubai - in store from Tuesday, February 2, 2027
Retail advisor training:
- Virtual sessions, 45 minutes each
- APAC sessions: Tuesday, December 1, 2026 and Thursday, December 3, 2026
- EMEA sessions: Tuesday, December 8, 2026 and Thursday, December 10, 2026
Training goals:
- Explain the product story in plain language
- Answer the five most common customer questions
- Use only the approved claims list
Approved claims (fictional):
- "Hydrates for up to 24 hours"
- "Suitable for daily use, morning and evening"
Not approved:
- Any medical claim, percentage result, or "clinically proven" wording
Open items:
- Seoul translation of training cards due Friday, November 20, 2026
- Dubai store count not yet confirmed
After you receive this source, reply only: "Got it. I'll use this as the source."
    Starting prompt (GCSE)▼
    Starting prompt - Goal, Context, Source, Expectation
    What this prompt doesTurns a short fact sheet into a briefing that stays inside the source and flags what is still unknown.
    Goal: Give me a short briefing on Project Solstice.
Context: I just joined the regional launch team and will attend the first APAC retail training session.
Source: Use only the pasted fact sheet. If something is not in it, say it is unknown.
Expectation: Give me five bullets, then a table with Region, Cities, In-store date, and Training dates, then a list of open items. Keep it under 200 words.
    Follow-ups (2)▼
    Follow-ups - same thread
    What this prompt doesReshapes the same facts for a different moment without adding anything new.
    Rewrite the briefing as three sentences I could say aloud at the start of a team call. Keep every date unchanged.
    What this prompt doesUses the gaps in the source to prepare better questions.
    Based only on the open items and gaps in the fact sheet, list up to four questions I should ask Mei Tanaka before the first APAC session.
    What to check▼
    Expected briefing: APAC in store on January 12, 2027 (Singapore, Sydney, Seoul); EMEA in store on February 2, 2027 (London, Paris, Dubai); APAC training December 1 and 3, 2026; EMEA training December 8 and 10, 2026; two approved claims only. The Dubai store count must stay not confirmed, and the Seoul translation stays due November 20, 2026. Remove anything Copilot added that is not in the fact sheet.
    Tip: Know where Copilot lives▼
    Microsoft Copilot Chat is available at copilot.cloud.microsoft, in the Microsoft Copilot app, in Microsoft Edge, and inside Outlook and Teams. Sign in with your ELC work account so your chats are covered by enterprise data protection.
    Did you know? Copilot Chat can work with content you give it▼
    Microsoft describes Copilot Chat as grounded in web data and able to work with content you paste, upload, or have open in Outlook and Teams. Grounding in your mailbox, files, and meetings requires a Microsoft 365 Copilot license. Microsoft Learn
    Recommended custom instructionStay inside my source▼

    This is a general working preference that is safe to save. Do not save project names, product details, or dates.

    When I paste a source, use only that source and label anything it does not cover as unknown.

    02

    Repair a weak prompt with GCSETurn a vague request into Goal, Context, Source, and Expectation.

    Copilot Chat
    What you'll practice · Who it's for▼
    What it does

    See how the same request improves when you state the goal, the context, the allowed source, and the expected output, and let Copilot ask questions when details are missing.

    Who it's for: Everyone who writes requests, messages, or plans
    1. Paste the practice source▼
    Paste this first - practice source
    Expected acknowledgmentAfter you paste the source, Copilot should reply only: “Got it. I'll use this as the source.”
    Open TXT
    FICTIONAL PROMPT REPAIR CARD
Owner: Lukas Becker, EMEA Retail Education Coordinator (fictional)
Weak prompt:
"Write something about the new serum training."
What Lukas actually needs:
- An invitation message for EMEA retail advisors
- Sessions: Tuesday, December 8, 2026 and Thursday, December 10, 2026
- Format: virtual, 45 minutes each
- Audience: advisors in London, Paris, and Dubai stores
- Tone: warm, clear, and short enough to read on a phone
- Must include: what advisors will learn and how to choose a session
- Must not include: product claims beyond the approved list
What advisors will learn:
- The Lumen Dew product story in plain language
- Answers to the five most common customer questions
Approved claims (fictional):
- "Hydrates for up to 24 hours"
- "Suitable for daily use, morning and evening"
Unknown details:
- Session start times (not yet confirmed)
- Registration link (not yet available)
After you receive this source, reply only: "Got it. I'll use this as the source."
    Starting prompt (GCSE)▼
    Starting prompt - Goal, Context, Source, Expectation
    What this prompt doesRebuilds a weak prompt with all four GCSE parts and invites Copilot to ask about missing details before drafting.
    Goal: Rewrite the weak prompt on the card as a strong GCSE prompt, then use it to draft the invitation.
Context: I coordinate retail education for EMEA and want a message advisors will read and act on.
Source: Use only the pasted prompt repair card.
Expectation: Show the improved prompt with labeled Goal, Context, Source, and Expectation lines. Then draft the invitation in under 120 words, use only approved claims, and use clear placeholders for start times and the registration link. Ask any clarifying questions before responding.
    Follow-ups (2)▼
    Follow-ups - same thread
    What this prompt doesAdapts the draft for a mobile chat message while keeping the guardrails.
    Shorten the invitation to under 60 words for a team chat message. Keep the placeholders and use only approved claims.
    What this prompt doesExplains what actually made the prompt better so you can repeat it.
    Compare the weak prompt and the improved prompt. In three bullets, explain which GCSE part changed the result the most and why.
    What to check▼
    Expected result: Copilot may first ask about start times, the registration link, or the preferred sign-off; answering with placeholders is fine. The invitation should name December 8 and December 10, 2026, say 45 minutes and virtual, name London, Paris, and Dubai, explain what advisors will learn, and use bracketed placeholders such as [start time] and [registration link]. It must not invent times or links or add claims beyond the two approved ones.
    Tip: Let Copilot ask first▼
    Adding “Ask any clarifying questions before responding.” gives Copilot permission to pause and request missing details instead of guessing. It reduces rework, but you still check the final draft. See the clarifying questions tip.
    Did you know? GCSE comes from Microsoft's own prompt guidance▼
    Microsoft's guidance says prompts can include four parts: Goal (what you want), Context (why and who is involved), Source (what information Copilot should use), and Expectations (how Copilot should respond). Only a clear goal is required. Microsoft Support
    Recommended custom instructionPlaceholders, not guesses▼

    A stable, nonsensitive preference that is safe to save.

    When details such as dates, times, names, or links are missing, use clear bracketed placeholders instead of inventing them.

    03

    Summarize an email thread and draft a replyPull commitments out of a thread and write a safe draft.

    Outlook
    What you'll practice · Who it's for▼
    What it does

    Use Copilot Chat in Outlook, or Copilot Chat in your browser, to find who committed to what and by when, then draft a reply you review before sending.

    Who it's for: Anyone who manages busy email threads across regions
    1. Paste the practice source▼
    Paste this first - practice source
    Expected acknowledgmentAfter you paste the source, Copilot should reply only: “Got it. I'll use this as the source.”
    Open TXT
    FICTIONAL EMAIL THREAD
Subject: Solstice APAC training - studio and materials
Message 1
From: Priya Nair, Training Operations, Singapore (fictional)
To: Mei Tanaka; Daniel Cho
Sent: Monday, November 16, 2026
Hi Mei, the Singapore studio is booked to record the December 1 session. I need the final slide files by Wednesday, November 25, 2026 to load them. Can you confirm who owns the Seoul translation?
Message 2
From: Daniel Cho, Retail Lead, Seoul (fictional)
To: Priya Nair; Mei Tanaka
Sent: Tuesday, November 17, 2026
I own the Seoul translation. Our vendor can deliver the Korean training cards on Friday, November 20, 2026 if we receive the final English text by Wednesday, November 18, 2026.
Message 3
From: Mei Tanaka, Regional Education Lead, APAC (fictional)
To: Priya Nair; Daniel Cho
Sent: Tuesday, November 17, 2026
Thanks both. I will send the final English text to Daniel by Wednesday, November 18. Priya, the slides will follow by Tuesday, November 24. Still open: Sydney asked whether the December 3 session can start one hour later. I have not decided.
After you receive this source, reply only: "Got it. I'll use this as the source."
    Starting prompt (GCSE)▼
    Starting prompt - Goal, Context, Source, Expectation
    What this prompt doesSeparates commitments from open questions and drafts a reply without making a decision that was never made.
    Goal: Summarize this email thread and draft my reply to Priya and Daniel.
Context: I am replying as Mei Tanaka, the fictional APAC education lead in this thread, and I want everyone aligned on dates.
Source: Use only the pasted email thread.
Expectation: Give a three-sentence summary, a table of commitments with Owner, Commitment, and Due date, and a list of open questions. Then draft a short reply that confirms the commitments. Keep the Sydney start-time question open. This is a draft only; do not imply it was sent.
    Follow-ups (2)▼
    Follow-ups - same thread
    What this prompt doesImproves tone and length while protecting the facts.
    Make the reply friendlier and under 80 words. Keep every date and owner unchanged.
    What this prompt doesLooks for the weak link in the plan.
    Using only the thread, explain what could cause the Korean training cards to miss Friday, November 20, 2026, and what I should watch for.
    What to check▼
    Expected commitments: Daniel owns the Seoul translation; the vendor delivers on November 20, 2026 only if the English text arrives by November 18, 2026. Mei sends the English text by November 18 and the slides by November 24. Priya needs the slides by November 25, and the studio is booked for the December 1 session. The Sydney request for a later December 3 start is undecided. Check that the reply does not decide it.
    Tip: Use the email you have open▼
    Copilot Chat in Outlook can work with the message you have open, so you do not always need to copy and paste. In this class, paste the fictional thread so everyone works from the same source. Optional for Microsoft 365 Copilot license holders: Copilot can also find and summarize mail across your mailbox, depending on your license and settings.
    Did you know? Verify before you send▼
    Microsoft's prompt guidance notes that Copilot is built on large language models, which are designed to predict and generate text. That is why a draft reply still needs a human check of names, dates, and commitments before it goes out. Microsoft Support
    Recommended custom instructionEmail reply style▼

    A stable writing preference. Never save recipients, thread details, or dates.

    When drafting email replies, keep them under 120 words, write dates as Month Day, Year, and list owners next to each action.

    Recap a Teams meetingSeparate decisions, actions, and open questions.

    Teams
    What you'll practice · Who it's for▼
    What it does

    Turn a meeting transcript into a recap for people who missed it, without turning ideas into decisions or assigning tasks nobody accepted.

    Who it's for: Meeting organizers, project leads, and anyone sharing follow-ups
    1. Paste the practice source▼
    Paste this first - practice source
    Expected acknowledgmentAfter you paste the source, Copilot should reply only: “Got it. I'll use this as the source.”
    Open TXT
    FICTIONAL TEAMS MEETING TRANSCRIPT
Meeting: Solstice EMEA training check-in
Date: Wednesday, November 18, 2026
Attendees (all fictional): Lukas Becker, EMEA Retail Education; Amélie Durand, Paris Store Trainer; Omar Haddad, Dubai Retail Manager; Sophie Clarke, London Training Lead
[00:01] Lukas: The goal today is to lock the EMEA session plan for December 8 and December 10.
[00:03] Sophie: London can host the December 8 session. I will test the Teams webinar link by Friday, November 27.
[00:06] Amélie: Paris advisors prefer December 10. I need French versions of the training cards by Monday, November 30.
[00:09] Omar: The Dubai store count is still not confirmed. I expect an answer from the regional office by Tuesday, November 24.
[00:12] Lukas: I will ask Mei whether the APAC translation vendor can also help with French.
[00:14] Amélie: Could we add a live product demo on camera?
[00:15] Lukas: Good idea, but we need to check it against the approved claims first. Let's not decide today.
[00:17] Lukas: Decision: two sessions. Sophie leads December 8 and Amélie leads December 10.
After you receive this source, reply only: "Got it. I'll use this as the source."
    Starting prompt (GCSE)▼
    Starting prompt - Goal, Context, Source, Expectation
    What this prompt doesCreates a recap that keeps decisions, accepted actions, and open ideas clearly apart.
    Goal: Recap this meeting for EMEA colleagues who missed it.
Context: I attended the meeting and need to post a clear recap in our team channel.
Source: Use only the pasted transcript.
Expectation: List decisions, then an action table with Owner, Action, and Due date, then open questions. Keep it under 200 words. Do not turn ideas into decisions, and leave a due date blank when none was given.
    Follow-ups (2)▼
    Follow-ups - same thread
    What this prompt doesAdapts the recap for one audience.
    Write a three-sentence Teams chat post from the recap for the Paris store team.
    What this prompt doesStress-tests the recap for misleading wording.
    List anything in the recap that could be misread as a decision, and quote the transcript line that shows its actual status.
    What to check▼
    Expected recap: The only decision is two sessions, with Sophie leading December 8 and Amélie leading December 10. Actions: Sophie tests the webinar link by November 27, 2026; Lukas asks Mei about French translation help (no due date). Open: French training cards needed by November 30, 2026 (no owner yet), the Dubai store count (answer expected by November 24, 2026), and the live product demo (not decided).
    Tip: Ideas are not decisions▼
    Meeting recaps often promote a suggestion into a decision. Ask Copilot to quote the line behind each decision and action, then check that no task was assigned to someone who did not accept it.
    Did you know? Organizers control Copilot in Teams meetings▼
    Microsoft documents settings that let Copilot in Teams meetings and events run during and after the meeting or only during the meeting. When organizers turn Copilot off for a meeting, recording and transcription are also turned off. Copilot in meetings depends on your license and policy. Microsoft Learn
    Recommended custom instructionRecap structure▼

    A stable format preference that is safe to save.

    For meeting recaps, separate decisions, actions with owners and due dates, and open questions. Do not invent owners or dates.

    Verify the output and catch errorsCheck a draft against its source before it reaches anyone.

    Copilot Chat
    What you'll practice · Who it's for▼
    What it does

    Practice responsible use by checking a polished-sounding draft against an approved source and correcting unsupported or wrong claims.

    Who it's for: Everyone who shares Copilot output with others
    1. Paste the practice source▼
    Paste this first - practice source
    Expected acknowledgmentAfter you paste the source, Copilot should reply only: “Got it. I'll use this as the source.”
    Open TXT
    FICTIONAL VERIFICATION PACKET
Part A - Approved source (fact sheet excerpt)
Product: Lumen Dew Hydrating Serum (fictional), 30 ml
APAC in-store date: Tuesday, January 12, 2027 (Singapore, Sydney, Seoul)
EMEA in-store date: Tuesday, February 2, 2027 (London, Paris, Dubai)
Approved claims:
- "Hydrates for up to 24 hours"
- "Suitable for daily use, morning and evening"
Dubai store count: not yet confirmed
Part B - Draft newsletter paragraph to check
"Lumen Dew Hydrating Serum arrives in Singapore, Sydney, and Seoul on January 12, 2027, followed by London, Paris, and Dubai on February 2, 2027. Clinically proven to reduce fine lines by 30%, it hydrates for up to 48 hours and is suitable for daily use. It will be available in all 40 Dubai stores."
After you receive this source, reply only: "Got it. I'll use this as the source."
    Starting prompt (GCSE)▼
    Starting prompt - Goal, Context, Source, Expectation
    What this prompt doesChecks each claim in a draft against an approved source and produces a corrected version.
    Goal: Check the draft paragraph in Part B against the approved source in Part A.
Context: I review content before it goes to retail teams and must catch anything unsupported.
Source: Treat Part A as the only source of truth and Part B as the draft under review.
Expectation: Make a table with each claim from Part B, a status of Supported, Not supported, or Contradicted, and the Part A evidence. Then write a corrected paragraph that uses only Part A. Do not add new facts.
    Follow-ups (2)▼
    Follow-ups - same thread
    What this prompt doesPrioritizes the errors by real-world risk.
    Rank the problems you found from highest to lowest risk if this paragraph reached customers, and explain each in one sentence.
    What this prompt doesTurns the lesson into a reusable habit.
    Write a five-item checklist I can reuse before sharing any Copilot draft with others.
    What to check▼
    Expected findings: The cities and both in-store dates are supported. “Clinically proven to reduce fine lines by 30%” is not supported and is not an approved claim. “Up to 48 hours” contradicts the approved “up to 24 hours.” “All 40 Dubai stores” is not supported because the store count is not confirmed. The corrected paragraph should also restore “morning and evening.”
    Tip: Check names, numbers, dates, and claims▼
    Fluent writing is not proof. Before you share a draft, compare every name, number, date, and claim with the source. If Copilot cannot point to evidence, remove it or mark it for review.
    Did you know? LLMs predict text one piece at a time▼
    Large language models break text into small units called tokens and learn to predict text based on its context. Prediction makes them fluent, not automatically correct, which is why verification stays your job. Microsoft Azure
    Recommended custom instructionFlag what needs checking▼

    A stable, nonsensitive preference that is safe to save.

    At the end of any draft, list the facts, numbers, and claims I should verify before sharing it.

    Prompting and safetyClear instructions; evidence before action.

    Core habits

    Copilot is built on large language models (LLMs). An LLM breaks text into small units called tokens and predicts likely text based on context. Grounding gives it the right source; a good prompt tells it what to do with that source. Microsoft prompt guidance · What are LLMs?

    Goal

    What do I want Copilot to do?

    Context

    Why do I need it, and who is the audience?

    Source

    Which information should Copilot use, and nothing else?

    Expectation

    What format, length, tone, and boundaries should the answer follow?

    Iterate instead of restarting. Stay in the same chat and ask Copilot to shorten, change the tone, show its evidence, list unknowns, or fix one weak GCSE line.
    Responsible use checklist▼
    • Sign in with your work account so enterprise data protection applies.
    • Share only the minimum information the task needs.
    • Do not paste personal, customer, or confidential information into an unapproved exercise.
    • Check every name, number, date, recipient, and claim against the source.
    • Use only approved product claims in anything customer-facing.
    • Review, edit, and own the final version. A draft never sends or approves anything.
    • Follow ELC policy and your tenant's controls; this guide does not replace them.

    Copilot tips & tricksUseful habits and exercise shortcuts.

    Quick reference
    Ask clarifying questions before Copilot answers▼

    Add “Ask any clarifying questions before responding.” when the answer depends on details you might have left out, such as audience, scope, source, format, or dates.

    This gives Copilot permission to pause, point out what is unclear, and ask for what it needs instead of silently filling gaps. It can reduce assumptions and rework, but it does not replace checking the source or reviewing the final output.

    Ask any clarifying questions before responding.
    Keep refining in the same chat▼

    Follow-ups build on what Copilot already knows from the conversation. Ask it to shorten, change the tone, or add a table instead of starting over. Start a new chat when you switch to an unrelated topic so old details do not leak into new work.

    Keep the same facts, but make this shorter, friendlier, and easier to read on a phone.
