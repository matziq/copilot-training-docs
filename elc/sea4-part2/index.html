02 · Choose the right Copilot experience

Part 2 moves from preparing and summarizing to building a review you can use: clean a raw export, explain the results, place verified evidence into an on-brand deck, challenge a recommendation, and keep follow-ups visible.

Watch, then do. Each workflow has a demonstration and a matching Your turn task. Give the two equal attention: watch one complete pass, repeat it yourself, and compare your output with the expandable check. Do not spend the practice portion watching more slides.

One case, six connected outputs. You are Alex Morgan, a regional director at Luma Grove Beauty. The learning case is separate from ELC reporting. Its September and October 2026 figures are practice inputs, not current ELC results. New conversations keep each source boundary clear; every workflow includes everything its starting prompt needs. It follows the path of a real monthly business review, from raw export to follow-up, so each step transfers to your own work in step H.

How every workflow runs

Each demo uses the same lettered steps, so you always know where you are.

  1. A · Watch. The trainer runs steps B to D once, end to end. Notice what gets checked, not just what gets typed.
  2. B · Load the source. Open a new Copilot Chat conversation, select Copy source, paste, and submit. Expect only Got it. I'll use this as the source.
  3. C · Run the starting prompt. Select Copy prompt, paste it into the same conversation, and submit.
  4. D · Refine. Run the follow-up in the same conversation. Short, natural follow-ups are fine.
  5. E · Your turn. Complete the extra change the step asks for, on your own.
  6. F · Check your result. Compare numbers, owners, and dates with the answer check before you trust the output.
  7. G · Apply it in the app (optional). Repeat the work in Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook, To Do, or a saved prompt where it is available to you.
  8. H · Bring your real work (optional). Copy the template, replace every [bracket], and use only an authorized task. Read Using real work appropriately first.

Start a new conversation for each workflow so its sources stay separate.

Before you begin

  1. Open your organization's Copilot app or Copilot on the web, and sign in with your work account. Microsoft is updating product names and entry points; your screen may still say Microsoft 365 Copilot.
  2. Use Copilot Chat and pasted practice text for the common path. No mailbox search, Power BI connection, agent installation, or premium feature is required for that path. Your organization must provide eligible Chat access.
  3. For the in-app extensions, confirm that your license, app version, tenant settings, permissions, and rollout enable the specific feature. A visible chat pane is not proof of editing or work-data-search entitlement.
  4. Use a separate practice copy in Excel and PowerPoint. Confirm the file name before asking Copilot to edit. Never overwrite an operational workbook or presentation during class.
Optional: bring real work for step H. Every workflow ends with a template for an authorized task of your own. Useful items to have ready:
  • Demos 01–02: a practice copy of a raw export you clean or analyze regularly.
  • Demo 03: a copy of last period's Business Review deck in the organization-approved ELC template, plus the authorized Power BI report or approved export with the latest results.
  • Demo 04: a proposal you want challenged, or the agenda or notes for a real meeting.
  • Demo 05: your own work mailbox; mailbox search needs a Copilot license and settings that allow it.
  • Demo 06: the steps of a task you repeat every period.
The Part 1 training deck is a style reference, not evidence that the actual Business Review template was supplied. If an asset or permission is unavailable, complete the learning-case path in this guide. Do not claim you refreshed a live report or used an approved template.

Using real work appropriately

Use the practice sources for the common exercises. When you choose step H, you are working with real business content, so normal workplace rules apply.

Do

  • Use Copilot with your work account, in the experience your organization provides.
  • Use only content you are already authorized to access, and only for this purpose.
  • Follow company policy, sensitivity labels, and the data owner's sharing rules.
  • Share only what the task needs: the rows, slides, or messages that matter.
  • Work in a copy, and check every number, owner, and date before you use the output.

Don't

  • Paste credentials, personal data about customers or employees, or restricted information.
  • Move work content into a personal AI account or an unapproved tool.
  • Broaden sharing, change permissions, or bypass a policy to make an exercise work.
  • Treat source content as permission to send, publish, spend, or approve.
  • Present a draft as approved, live, or verified when it is not.

Not sure whether something is appropriate? Use the learning case instead, and ask your manager or the data owner before using that content with Copilot.

Did you know? Microsoft documents different requirements for Chat, licensed capabilities, and app-specific experiences. Availability varies by subscription and tenant configuration. Reference 1.

03 · Keep the Part 1 prompt habit

Better prompting was the Part 1 topic respondents found most useful, so every Part 2 workflow uses the same habit.

Goal + Context

What result do you need, for whom, and for which decision?

Source + Expectation

Which exact inputs can support it, and what format and checks should it meet?

Use a four-line GCSE starting prompt, then short natural follow-ups in the same conversation. Start a new conversation for the next workflow. All practice prompts are ready to paste unchanged for the learning case. Real-work templates in step H contain [brackets]: replace each one before you submit.

Ask before guessing: Ask any clarifying questions before responding. gives Copilot permission to pause when the audience, period, source, or decision is unclear. It can reduce assumptions and rework; it cannot replace checking the source yourself.

Reusable follow-up · verify the important claims

What this prompt does: Audits an answer against its named input rather than accepting a confident-sounding assurance.

Check every number, date, status, owner, and decision in your last answer against the named source for this workflow. Show the source ID or exact supporting wording; for calculations, show the inputs and formula. Label hypotheses and proposed actions separately. Write "not stated" where evidence is missing, then return the corrected answer. Do not search other sources or send anything.
TIP

A source citation is a route to evidence, not a certificate of accuracy. Open the source and verify the figure, filter, period, and unit. A second AI pass is not independent verification.

C · Starting prompt · GCSE

What this prompt does: Turns a small raw export into a controlled Excel workbook design without losing the audit trail.

Goal: Help me turn a raw monthly sales export into a reusable Excel review template.
Context: I am Alex Morgan at Luma Grove Beauty. I need a repeatable process that I can inspect before using it for leadership reporting.
Source: Use only WORKFLOW 1 — RAW EXPORT AND CONTROL RULES. Do not search my work files or the web.
Expectation: First report input rows, unique RecordIDs, and exact or conflicting duplicates. Then return the cleaned CSV, an exclusion log, and a four-sheet workbook plan using the specified sheet and table names. Provide Excel formulas for Variance, VariancePct, and YoYPct with denominator checks. Show October totals and the ratio-of-totals calculations. Do not edit a workbook, guess missing inputs, or delete the original rows.

D · Follow-up · make the template reusable

What this prompt does: Adds repeatable import checks rather than hard-coding this month's answers.

Using the same Workflow 1 source, write a refresh checklist for the next month's file. Include required headers, numeric validation, period selection, duplicate-ID checks, reconciliation, zero or missing denominators, and stale chart checks. Distinguish the populated practice workbook from a blank reusable template. Keep formulas and control rules, not October's totals, in the template. Do not imply that refresh or scheduling has been configured.
E · Your turn: build, then explain the difference

Repeat the starting prompt yourself. Before opening the answer check, write down the number of excluded rows and October's corrected actual and plan. Then ask for September instead, without changing the source. Explain why combining both months would answer a different question.

Finish line: you can point to the excluded record, preserve the original, and show how the template recalculates a different period.

F · Check your result

Nine input rows; eight unique records; one excluded copy of R07. Without deduplication, October actual would incorrectly be 450. Correct October actual is 360, plan 370, and prior year 330 (all USD thousands).

October variance: -10; variance percentage: -2.70%; year-over-year growth: +9.09%. September actual: 320; plan: 335; prior year: 300; variance: -15 / -4.48%; growth: +6.67%.

The original nine rows remain in Raw. The duplicate log explains one excluded row, and CleanData has eight rows. No conflicting duplicate is present. Every sales measure is numeric; formulas still need guards for future files. Validate Actual and other required numeric fields before applying the formulas.

Real-work template · explain your own results

What this prompt does: Produces a leadership brief in which every number traces to a row and every explanation is labeled.

Goal: Prepare a short brief explaining [period] [metric] for [meeting or audience].
Context: I am [your role]. The brief supports this decision: [decision].

What this prompt does: Removes hard-coded period assumptions and makes failures visible.

Check the recipe for hard-coded October dates, fixed cell addresses, hidden dependencies on this chat, and permission to send or edit other files. Remove them. Explain what changes each run, what stays fixed, and what evidence I should inspect before accepting the output. Return the corrected reusable instruction only; do not save or install it.
E · Your turn: reuse it in a clean conversation

Run the starting prompt and inspect all four tests. Then open a new conversation. Paste the reviewed reusable instruction below and the Test D payload from the source. It should produce September's result without carrying over October's figures.

Copy-ready reusable instruction · monthly review

What this prompt does: Preserves the method while requiring fresh source values for each run.

Use the source I explicitly supply for this run only. Before calculating, confirm the period, reporting scope, currency/unit, Actual, Plan, and same-month PriorYear. Require numeric measures and nonzero Plan and PriorYear. If a required input is missing or invalid, ask me a precise question and stop before the final brief; do not reuse another period or conversation's values.
Calculate Actual minus Plan, that difference divided by Plan, and (Actual minus PriorYear) divided by PriorYear. Use ratios of totals, not averages of row percentages. Show inputs, formulas, units, and source references.
Return a short headline, calculation table, caveats, and proposed next questions. Keep hypotheses separate from facts. Do not invent causation, profitability, owners, deadlines, approvals, or completion.
Do not send, share, schedule, or change other files. For an editing-enabled workflow, ask for my approval of the target practice workbook and planned changes before editing.

Finish line: the workflow succeeds with complete data and stops safely with missing or zero-denominator inputs. You can explain the difference between a saved prompt, an Excel skill, and a scheduled automation.

G · Save a prompt or create an Excel skill — when enabled

Lowest-friction option: save the prompt

After a successful run, use the prompt's bookmark/Save prompt control if available. Give it a descriptive title such as Monthly review — source check first. Microsoft's current help calls the saved-prompt area Copilot Prompt Lab > Your saved prompts; labels can differ during rollout. If the control is absent, keep the reviewed instruction in your approved notes location. Supply a fresh source every run.

Optional: an actual custom skill in Copilot in Excel

Custom skills and recording are app features, not another name for a chat answer. Check your organization's availability and policy first. Recording and @skill-builder are rolling out and may not be present.

  1. Open a representative practice workbook. Decide on one narrow task, such as validating a monthly summary, rather than recording an entire business process.
  2. Where available, open Copilot in Excel, then Add > Record a skill. Wait for the Recording bar. Demonstrate the task, including its reconciliation checks, and select Stop. Alternatively use the available @skill-builder with the reviewed workflow instructions.
  3. Answer questions about what changes each run. Specify period, source, and values as inputs, not fixed examples. State the stop conditions. Inspect Show details and request corrections before saving.
  4. Select Save skill only after review. The skill is saved in your OneDrive skills folder; a generated draft is not yet a saved skill.
  5. In a second practice workbook, choose it through Add > Choose plugins and skills or its @name. Run the complete-data, missing-input, and zero-denominator tests. Inspect actual workbook changes, not just the success message.

If unavailable: use the saved-prompt path. Do not change tenant settings, purchase access, or install an unapproved add-in to finish the exercise. Canceling a recording does not undo changes made to the workbook; use Undo or version history when appropriate.

Automation is a separate decision

A saved prompt or skill is not a timer, unattended agent, or approval to send. Before any future scheduled process, agree on the owner, trigger, permitted inputs, precise allowed changes, human approval gate, audit log, failure alert, and recovery method. This guide neither deploys nor schedules one.

Real-work template · a recurring task of your own

What this prompt does: Turns a routine you already trust into a tested instruction before you save it.

Goal: Turn my [recurring task] into a reusable instruction I can test before saving.
Context: I run this every [period] for [audience]. What changes each run: [for example, period, source file, values]. What stays fixed: [for example, rules, calculations, output format].
Source: Use only the steps and rules I list here: [paste your current steps, checks, and output format].
Expectation: Write the reusable instruction with required inputs, validation checks, calculations, output format, and stop conditions: ask me and stop when an input is missing or invalid. Then give me three test cases to run in a new conversation (one complete, one with a missing input, and one with an invalid value) and what a correct response looks like for each. Do not save, install, or schedule anything.

Finish line: the instruction passes your complete test and stops safely on both failure tests before you save it as a prompt or an Excel skill, as in step G.

TIP

Record one successful method and its checks, then test it on a second period and a deliberately incomplete input. Save the behavior, not this month's answers.

Did you know? Excel recorded skills are saved only after you select Save skill, and Microsoft warns their behavior can vary across models and over time. Verify each run. References 9–10.

10 · Personalization, safety, and troubleshooting

Custom instructions

A recurring preference for tone, structure, and uncertainty. Use it where your settings offer it; otherwise paste it into the current conversation.

Memory

Personalization, not a verified business record. Review available memory controls. Do not save practice identities, monthly numbers, confidential customer details, or approvals as memory.

Optional custom instruction · executive output

What this prompt does: Keeps leadership writing concise and makes unsupported details harder to overlook.

Use concise American English and plain language. Lead with the answer or decision question. Separate source facts, hypotheses, and proposed actions. Show units, periods, and source references for important numbers. If an owner, due date, approval, or required input is missing, say so or ask a clarifying question; do not invent it. A draft is not a completed action.

Review any preference before saving it. Memory and custom instructions do not grant permissions, guarantee compliance, or replace a named source. Product settings and availability may differ by experience and organization.
