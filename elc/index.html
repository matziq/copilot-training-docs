    PROJECT JUNIPER - FACT SHEET
Holiday gift set sampling pilot
Prepared by: Priya Natarajan, Program Lead, Consumer Engagement
Prepared for: Jordan Avery, Project Coordinator (joined the team on Wednesday, November 11, 2026)

- Brand ambassador staffing: USD 30,000
- Display fixtures: USD 12,000
Success measures:

- At least 2,000 new loyalty sign-ups across the pilot stores
- Average customer survey score of 4.3 or higher out of 5
Team and owners:
- Priya Natarajan, Program Lead: overall pilot and weekly status report
- Marcus Bell, Retail Operations: store list and ambassador scheduling
- Elena Ruiz, Finance: budget tracking
- Tom Okafor, Brand Marketing: signage and sample kit design
- Grace Lin, Regulatory Affairs: approval of product claims on signage
Key dates:
- Friday, November 20, 2026: approved signage claims due (Grace Lin)
- Monday, November 23, 2026: sample kits arrive at the regional warehouses (Marcus Bell)
- Wednesday, November 25, 2026: displays installed in all 12 stores (Marcus Bell)
- Friday, November 27, 2026: pilot starts (Priya Natarajan)
- Friday, January 15, 2027: results readout to leadership (Priya Natarajan)

After you receive this source, reply only: "Got it. I'll use this as the source."
    B. Starting prompt (GCSE)▼
    Starting prompt - Goal, Context, Source, Expectation
    What this prompt doesAsks Copilot Chat for a short briefing that uses only the fact sheet you pasted. Useful for anyone who joins a project, covers for a colleague, or prepares for a first meeting.
    Goal: Give me a short briefing on Project Juniper.
Context: I just joined the pilot team as project coordinator, and I meet Priya, the program lead, tomorrow.
Source: Use only the pasted fact sheet. If something is not in it, write "Not in the source."
Expectation: Five bullets, a table of key dates and owners, and a short list of open items, under 200 words.
    C. Follow-ups (2)▼
    Follow-ups - same thread
    What this prompt doesTurns the briefing into preparation for a real conversation, still grounded in the same fact sheet.
    Which three questions should I ask Priya tomorrow? Base each question on an open item or a key date in the fact sheet.
    What this prompt doesChanges the format and audience without changing the facts.
    Rewrite the briefing as three sentences I could say out loud to my manager. Keep every number exactly as it appears in the fact sheet.
    D. What to check▼
    • Dates: the pilot runs Friday, November 27, 2026 through Thursday, December 24, 2026, and the results readout is Friday, January 15, 2027.
    • Numbers: 12 stores (Northeast 5, Midwest 4, West 3), 18,000 sample kits (1,500 per store), and a USD 96,000 budget (USD 54,000 + USD 30,000 + USD 12,000).
    • Owners: Grace Lin owns the signage claims due Friday, November 20, 2026. Marcus Bell owns the kit arrival on Monday, November 23 and the display installation on Wednesday, November 25, 2026.
    • Open items: the third West store, the ambassador training date, and the Canada decision. Copilot should not say the pilot is expanding to Canada.
    • Anything the fact sheet does not cover, such as a training date, should say "Not in the source" rather than a guess.
    E. Tip: Know where Copilot lives▼
    Microsoft Copilot Chat is available at copilot.cloud.microsoft, in the Microsoft 365 Copilot app, in Microsoft Edge, and inside Outlook and Teams. Sign in with your ELC work account so your chats are covered by enterprise data protection. If you are not sure which Copilot you have, follow the core steps in this guide; they work with Copilot Chat.
    F. Did you know? Copilot Chat can work with content you give it▼
    Microsoft describes Copilot Chat as grounded in web data and able to work with content you paste, upload, or have open in Outlook and Teams. Grounding in your mailbox, files, and meetings requires a Microsoft 365 Copilot license. Microsoft Learn
    G. Recommended custom instructionSource-only answers▼

    A stable, nonsensitive working preference. Save it only if custom instructions are available to you and allowed by your organization.

    When I paste a source, use only that source and write "Not in the source" for anything it does not cover.

    Repair a weak prompt with GCSETurn a vague request into a clear one.

    Copilot Chat
    A. Paste the practice source▼

    Open Copilot Chat with your ELC work account and select New chat for this demo. Copy the source below, paste it into the chat, and send it. After the acknowledgment, paste the prompt in B into the same chat; then try the follow-ups in C.

    Paste this first - practice source
    Expected acknowledgmentAfter you paste the source, Copilot should reply only: “Got it. I'll use this as the source.”
    Open TXT
    PROJECT JUNIPER - STORE MANAGER NOTE DETAILS
From: Jordan Avery, Project Coordinator
Audience: Store managers at the 12 pilot stores
The weak first prompt I tried: "Write an email about the pilot."
What store managers need to know:
- Sample kits arrive at each store by Tuesday, November 24, 2026: 1,500 kits per store
- Displays are installed by Wednesday, November 25, 2026
- Sampling runs Friday, November 27, 2026 through Thursday, December 24, 2026
- Brand ambassadors staff each store Friday through Sunday during the pilot
- Stores record loyalty sign-ups daily in the existing loyalty tablet app
- Questions go to Marcus Bell, Retail Operations
Not yet confirmed:
- Brand ambassador training date
- The third West region store
After you receive this source, reply only: "Got it. I'll use this as the source."
    B. Starting prompt (GCSE)▼
    Starting prompt - Goal, Context, Source, Expectation
    What this prompt doesRebuilds the vague request "Write an email about the pilot" into a complete GCSE prompt and invites Copilot to ask questions first. Useful for anyone who writes announcements, updates, or instructions for another team.
    Goal: Draft a short note to store managers about the Project Juniper sampling pilot.
Context: I'm the project coordinator, and store managers need to prepare their teams before the pilot starts.
Source: Use only the pasted details. Do not add dates, quantities, or promises that are not there.
Expectation: A subject line and fewer than 150 words, with dates in a bulleted list. Mark anything not yet confirmed as "to be confirmed." Ask any clarifying questions before responding.
    C. Follow-ups (2)▼
    Follow-ups - same thread
    What this prompt doesAnswers Copilot's clarifying questions in plain language, so the draft reflects your choices instead of its guesses.
    Here are my answers: send it from me, Jordan Avery; keep the tone friendly and practical; and name Marcus Bell as the contact for questions. Now write the note.
    What this prompt doesShows why the GCSE version worked better, so you can repair your own weak prompts later.
    Compare your note with my first prompt, "Write an email about the pilot." In one line each, explain what the Goal, Context, Source, and Expectation added.
    D. What to check▼
    • A subject line, fewer than 150 words, and the dates in a bulleted list.
    • Dates and quantities: 1,500 kits per store by Tuesday, November 24, 2026; displays by Wednesday, November 25, 2026; sampling from Friday, November 27 through Thursday, December 24, 2026.
    • The ambassador training date and the third West store are marked "to be confirmed" instead of receiving an invented date or store name.
    • Marcus Bell is the contact for questions. The note makes no promises about incentives, budgets, or Canada.
    • If Copilot asked clarifying questions before drafting, that is the expected behavior, not an error.
    E. Tip: Let Copilot ask first▼
    Adding "Ask any clarifying questions before responding." gives Copilot permission to pause and request missing details instead of guessing. It reduces rework, but you still check the final draft. See the clarifying questions tip.
    F. Did you know? GCSE comes from Microsoft's own prompt guidance▼
    Microsoft's guidance says prompts can include four parts: Goal (what you want), Context (why and who is involved), Source (what information Copilot should use), and Expectations (how Copilot should respond). Only a clear goal is required. Microsoft Support
    G. Recommended custom instructionAsk before drafting▼

    A stable working preference that helps across many tasks.

    When my request is missing the audience, format, or source, ask me up to three clarifying questions before you draft.

    Outlook / Copilot Chat
    A. Paste the practice source▼

    Open Copilot Chat with your ELC work account and select New chat for this demo. Copy the source below, paste it into the chat, and send it. After the acknowledgment, paste the prompt in B into the same chat; then try the follow-ups in C. If Copilot Chat is available in your Outlook ribbon, you can paste the same source into that Copilot pane instead. The practice thread is supplied below; do not search your mailbox for it. Review the reply as a draft; do not send it.

    Paste this first - practice source
    Expected acknowledgmentAfter you paste the source, Copilot should reply only: “Got it. I'll use this as the source.”
    Open TXT
    EMAIL THREAD - Subject: Project Juniper - Midwest kit delivery
Message 1
From: Marcus Bell, Retail Operations
To: Jordan Avery; Elena Ruiz
Sent: Monday, November 16, 2026, 9:12 AM
Hi both, the carrier says the Midwest delivery may slip from Monday, November 23 to Wednesday, November 25, 2026. That would leave only two days before the pilot starts. I will confirm the new delivery date by Wednesday, November 18. Elena, can we pay for expedited shipping if we need it?
Message 2
From: Elena Ruiz, Finance
To: Marcus Bell; Jordan Avery
Sent: Monday, November 16, 2026, 11:40 AM
The carrier's phone estimate for expedited shipping is USD 2,400. I can approve up to USD 2,500 from the contingency line once I have the quote in writing. Marcus, can you send me the written quote?
Message 3
From: Priya Natarajan, Program Lead
To: Marcus Bell; Elena Ruiz; Jordan Avery
Sent: Monday, November 16, 2026, 2:05 PM
Thanks, both. Jordan, please draft a reply to everyone that confirms who is doing what and by when. One question is still open: if the kits arrive late, do the four Midwest stores start sampling on Saturday, November 28 instead? I have not decided yet. I will decide after Marcus confirms the delivery date.
After you receive this source, reply only: "Got it. I'll use this as the source."
    B. Starting prompt (GCSE)▼
    Starting prompt - Goal, Context, Source, Expectation
    What this prompt doesPulls the commitments out of a busy thread and drafts a reply that you review before sending. Useful for anyone who coordinates work across teams by email.
    Goal: Summarize this email thread and draft my reply to everyone on it.
Context: I'm Jordan Avery, the project coordinator, and Priya asked me to confirm who is doing what and by when.
Source: Use only the pasted thread. Do not decide the open question or add commitments that nobody made.
Expectation: First, a table with columns for Person, Commitment, Due date, and Status (Committed, Requested, or Open). Then a reply under 120 words that keeps the open question open.
    C. Follow-ups (2)▼
    Follow-ups - same thread
    What this prompt doesAsks Copilot to show its evidence so you can check each row quickly.
    Which sentence in the thread supports each row of the table? Quote the exact sentence for each row.
    What this prompt doesTightens the draft and adds a request in your own voice without turning anything into a decision.
    Make the reply more concise, and add one clear ask: Marcus, please send Elena the written carrier quote. Keep it as a draft for me to review.
    D. What to check▼
    • Marcus Bell: confirm the new Midwest delivery date by Wednesday, November 18, 2026 (Committed).
    • Elena Ruiz: approve expedited shipping, estimated at USD 2,400, up to USD 2,500 from the contingency line once she has the written quote (Committed, with a condition).
    • Marcus Bell: send Elena the written carrier quote (Requested; Marcus has not replied yet).
    • Priya Natarajan: decide whether the four Midwest stores start on Saturday, November 28, 2026 (Open; she will decide after Marcus confirms the date).
    • The reply must not say that the Midwest stores will start late or that expedited shipping is approved. Copilot drafts; you review and send.
    E. Tip: Use the email you have open▼
    Copilot Chat in Outlook can work with the message you have open, so you do not always need to copy and paste. In class, paste the practice thread so everyone works from the same source. Optional for Microsoft 365 Copilot license holders: Copilot can also find and summarize mail across your mailbox, depending on your license and settings.
    F. Did you know? Verify before you send▼
    Microsoft's prompt guidance notes that Copilot is built on large language models, which are designed to predict and generate text. That is why a draft reply still needs a human check of names, dates, and commitments before it goes out. Microsoft Support
    G. Recommended custom instructionEmail reply style▼

    A stable, nonsensitive style preference for drafts you always review.

    When you draft email replies for me, keep them under 120 words, use a short greeting, and end with clear next steps, owners, and dates.

    Teams / Copilot Chat
    A. Paste the practice source▼

    Open Copilot Chat with your ELC work account and select New chat for this demo. Copy the source below, paste it into the chat, and send it. After the acknowledgment, paste the prompt in B into the same chat; then try the follow-ups in C. You can also use the Copilot Chat app in Teams if your organization has made it available.     Open TXT
    TEAMS MEETING TRANSCRIPT EXCERPT
Meeting: Project Juniper weekly sync
Date: Tuesday, November 17, 2026, 10:00 AM ET
Attendees: Priya Natarajan, Marcus Bell, Elena Ruiz, Tom Okafor, Grace Lin, Jordan Avery
[10:01] Priya Natarajan: Let's start with signage. Grace, where are we on the claims review?
[10:02] Grace Lin: I approved four of the five signage claims. The claim "visibly brighter skin in 7 days" is not approved because it needs supporting test data. Tom, please remove it from the signage.
[10:03] Tom Okafor: Understood. I'll send the revised signage files to Marcus by Thursday, November 19.
[10:05] Marcus Bell: The third West store is still open. I could ask the Portland store, but I haven't contacted them yet.
[10:06] Priya Natarajan: Marcus, please contact the Portland store and report back by Friday, November 20.
[10:06] Marcus Bell: Will do.
[10:08] Elena Ruiz: Budget check: we have committed USD 81,500 of the USD 96,000 budget so far.
[10:10] Tom Okafor: Idea: maybe we add a gift-with-purchase card to every bag?
[10:11] Priya Natarajan: Interesting, but let's not decide today. It would need a budget check first.
[10:12] Jordan Avery: For ambassador training, how about Monday, November 23?
[10:13] Marcus Bell: That might work. I need to check the ambassador agency's availability first.
[10:15] Priya Natarajan: Decision: we will send a results report every Monday, starting Monday, November 30. Jordan, can you set up the report template?
[10:15] Jordan Avery: Yes. I'll have the template ready by Wednesday, November 25.
After you receive this source, reply only: "Got it. I'll use this as the source."
    B. Starting prompt (GCSE)▼
    Starting prompt - Goal, Context, Source, Expectation
    What this prompt doesTurns a meeting transcript into a recap that keeps decisions, action items, and ideas apart. Useful for anyone who runs, attends, or follows up on team meetings.
    Goal: Write a recap of this Project Juniper meeting.
Context: I'm sharing it with the six attendees, and it must be accurate about what was decided versus only discussed.
Source: Use only the pasted transcript excerpt, and quote the line behind each decision and action item.
Expectation: Three headings: Decisions; Action items (owner, task, due date); and Open items and ideas. Under 200 words. Do not turn ideas into decisions.
    C. Follow-ups (2)▼
    Follow-ups - same thread
    What this prompt doesChecks for the most common recap error: treating a suggestion as an assignment.
    Did anyone get an action item they did not accept? List anything that is only a suggestion or still needs confirmation.
    What this prompt doesTurns the checked recap into a quick follow-up that you review and post yourself.
    Draft a short Teams chat message to the attendees with the three action items and a reminder that the gift-with-purchase card is not decided. Keep it under 80 words.
    D. What to check▼
    • Decisions: a results report goes out every Monday, starting Monday, November 30, 2026. The claim "visibly brighter skin in 7 days" is not approved and comes off the signage.
    • Action items: Tom Okafor sends the revised signage files to Marcus by Thursday, November 19, 2026. Marcus Bell contacts the Portland store and reports back by Friday, November 20, 2026. Jordan Avery has the report template ready by Wednesday, November 25, 2026.
    • Open items and ideas: the gift-with-purchase card (an idea that needs a budget check), ambassador training on Monday, November 23, 2026 (proposed; Marcus must check the agency's availability), and the third West store.
    • Budget: USD 81,500 of USD 96,000 is committed. If Copilot calculates what remains, it should be USD 14,500.
    • No idea or proposal appears under Decisions.
    E. Tip: Ideas are not decisions▼
    Meeting recaps often promote a suggestion into a decision. Ask Copilot to quote the line behind each decision and action, then check that no task was assigned to someone who did not accept it.
    F. Did you know? Organizers control Copilot in Teams meetings▼
    Microsoft documents settings that let Copilot in Teams meetings and events run during and after the meeting or only during the meeting. When organizers turn Copilot off for a meeting, recording and transcription are also turned off. Copilot in meetings depends on your license and policy. Microsoft Learn
    G. Recommended custom instructionMeeting recap format▼

    A stable format preference; keep meeting content itself in the chat.

    For meeting recaps, use three headings: Decisions, Action items with owner and due date, and Open items. Never list an idea as a decision.

    A. Paste the practice source▼

    Paste this first - practice source
    Expected acknowledgmentAfter you paste the source, Copilot should reply only: “Got it. I'll use this as the source.”
    Open TXT
    PART A - APPROVED PROJECT JUNIPER FACTS (as of Wednesday, November 18, 2026)
- Pilot dates: Friday, November 27, 2026 through Thursday, December 24, 2026
- Pilot stores: 12 partner stores (Northeast 5, Midwest 4, West 3)
- Sample kits: 18,000 (1,500 per store)
- Budget: USD 96,000 total
- Success measure: gift set sell-through at least 15% higher than in matched non-pilot stores
- Approved signage claim: "A winter ritual for radiant-looking skin"
- Not approved: "visibly brighter skin in 7 days"
- Results readout to leadership: Friday, January 15, 2027
- Extending the pilot to Canada: not decided
PART B - DRAFT ANNOUNCEMENT TO CHECK
From: Jordan Avery, Project Coordinator
1. Project Juniper launches on Friday, November 27, 2026, in 15 partner stores.
2. Each store receives 1,500 deluxe sample kits.
3. The pilot runs through Thursday, December 24, 2026.
4. Our signage promises visibly brighter skin in 7 days.
5. Our goal is a 25% lift in gift set sell-through.
6. The pilot will expand to Canada in January 2027.
7. Results go to leadership on Friday, January 15, 2027.
8. Shoppers who sample the gift set love it.
After you receive this source, reply only: "Got it. I'll use this as the source."
    B. Starting prompt (GCSE)▼
    Starting prompt - Goal, Context, Source, Expectation
    What this prompt doesUses Copilot as a second reader that checks a draft against approved facts. Useful for anyone who shares numbers, dates, or product claims with others.
    Goal: Check the draft announcement in Part B against the approved facts in Part A.
Context: I'm about to send this announcement to the wider team, and every claim must match the approved facts.
Source: Use only Part A as evidence. Treat Part B as the text to check, not as a source.
Expectation: A table with columns for Claim number, Claim, Verdict (Supported, Not supported, or Contradicted), and Evidence from Part A. Then list the corrections I need to make.
    C. Follow-ups (2)▼
    Follow-ups - same thread
    What this prompt doesProduces a corrected draft that you still review before sending.
    Rewrite the announcement using only supported claims and the corrections. Remove anything that cannot be supported, and keep it under 100 words.
    What this prompt doesHelps you prioritize: product claims and commitments often carry more risk than a typo.
    Which claim would be riskiest to send uncorrected, and why? Answer in two sentences.
    D. What to check▼
    • Supported: claims 2, 3, and 7.
    • Contradicted: claim 1 (12 stores, not 15; the launch date is correct), claim 4 (that signage claim is not approved), claim 5 (the target is 15%, not 25%), and claim 6 (the Canada extension is not decided).
    • Not supported: claim 8. Part A has no evidence about shopper reactions, and the pilot has not started.
    • The rewrite uses the approved claim "A winter ritual for radiant-looking skin" or leaves signage out, and it does not mention Canada or shopper reactions.
    • For the riskiest claim, an unapproved product claim (claim 4) is a strong answer; an unapproved commitment to expand (claim 6) is also reasonable.
    E. Tip: Check names, numbers, dates, and claims▼
    Fluent writing is not proof. Before you share a draft, compare every name, number, date, and claim with the source. If Copilot cannot point to evidence, remove the claim or mark it for review.
    F. Did you know? LLMs predict text one piece at a time▼
    Large language models break text into small units called tokens and learn to predict text based on its context. Prediction makes them fluent, not automatically correct, which is why verification stays your job. Microsoft Azure
    G. Recommended custom instructionEvidence first▼

    A stable working preference for any fact-checking request.

    When I ask you to check a draft, label each claim Supported, Not supported, or Contradicted, and quote the evidence you used.

    Prompting and safetyClear instructions; evidence before action.

    Core habits
    Where to find Copilot: copilot.cloud.microsoft, the Microsoft 365 Copilot app, Microsoft Edge, and Copilot Chat inside Outlook and Teams. Sign in with your ELC work account so enterprise data protection applies to your chats.

    Copilot is built on large language models (LLMs). An LLM breaks text into small units called tokens and predicts likely text based on context. Grounding gives it the right source, such as a fact sheet, an email thread, or a meeting transcript; a good prompt tells it what to do with that source. Microsoft prompt guidance · What are LLMs?

    Goal

    What do I want Copilot to do?

    Context

    Why do I need it, and who is the audience?

    Source

    Which information should Copilot use, and nothing else?

    Expectation

    What format, length, tone, and boundaries should the answer follow?

    Iterate instead of restarting. Stay in the same chat and ask Copilot to quote its evidence, shorten, change the audience, list unknowns, or fix one weak GCSE line.

    Copilot tips & tricksUseful habits and exercise shortcuts.

    Quick reference
    Ask clarifying questions before Copilot answers▼

    Add "Ask any clarifying questions before responding." when the answer depends on details you might have left out, such as audience, scope, source, format, or dates.

    This gives Copilot permission to pause, point out what is unclear, and ask for what it needs instead of silently filling gaps. It can reduce assumptions and rework, but it does not replace checking the source or reviewing the final output.

    Ask any clarifying questions before responding.
    Keep refining in the same chat▼

    Follow-ups build on what Copilot already knows from the conversation. Ask it to shorten, change the audience, or quote its evidence instead of starting over. Start a new chat when you switch to an unrelated topic so old details do not leak into new work.

    Keep the same facts, but make this shorter and easier to read on a phone.
